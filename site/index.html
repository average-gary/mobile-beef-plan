Shenandoah Valley mobile beef harvest & processing

A custom-exempt mobile beef service for Virginia's northern Shenandoah Valley. A mobile unit harvests cattle on the owner's farm, the carcass hangs and ages in a company-owned cooler, and the same unit then cuts, vacuum-packs and labels the beef while docked at the cooler. Customers are cattle owners (freezer-beef producers) and the households that buy their animals in halves and quarters before slaughter.

The model in 5 steps

  1. Booking. The owner books a date and signs the cut sheet. For shared animals, the list of owners is recorded before slaughter (a VDACS requirement).
  2. Harvest on the farm. The mobile unit kills, skins and eviscerates on the owner's farm. Blood is captured, not spread. The carcass chills in the unit's onboard cooler.
  3. Hang and age in the company cooler. Sides or quarters hang 7–21 days at about 34°F.
  4. Cut and pack at the cooler dock. The mobile unit docks at the cooler to cut to the owner's sheet, grind, vacuum-pack, label "Not for Sale" and freeze.
  5. Owner pickup at the company cooler, with optional delivery.

Custom-exempt beef is marked "Not for Sale": it cannot be sold at retail, to restaurants, at farmers' markets or online. Customers must own the live animal before slaughter. VDACS expressly allows a custom-exempt "mobile slaughter and processing unit" under a free annual Custom Permit.

Why now

  • Regional USDA plants ran at 125–150% of normal capacity in 2021, with producer waits of 12–18 months (PEC / American Farmland Trust / Fuller Consulting).
  • Virginia's own five-year plan (VDACS RD894, 2023) records 18–24 month waits, backs investment in custom-exempt capacity, and notes that the whole state depends on a single renderer.
  • The nearest USDA plant to Winchester still books 3–5 months out in 2026.
  • The documented reason mobile units fail is the lack of a partner to hang and cut their carcasses; owning the cooler and the cutting removes that dependency.

Three things decide go / no-go

1. Zoning for the cooler site

In Frederick County's Rural Areas (RA) district, any place where meat is "processed" is a slaughterhouse use needing a conditional use permit: 100 ft setbacks, all operations under roof, screening and public hearings. A commercially zoned parcel with public sewer may be simpler.

2. Wastewater

Blood and wash water are regulated "industrial wastes". Blood capture plus public sewer at the cooler is the clean route; DEQ's position on wash water at farm kills is still an open question.

3. Equipment cost and labor

The kill-and-cut unit lists from $300,000 (Sentinel, 2026) and other builders quote only. Cutting takes about 8 labor-hours per custom beef, so two people top out near 300–400 head a year and a second skilled cutter is essential.
