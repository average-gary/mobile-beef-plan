# NMPAN: which public money actually works for small meat processors

## Extracted facts (verbatim where quoted)

- "Many financial assistance programs at the state and federal levels will not work for small meat processors. If we had a dollar for every program out there that we were told could help small meat processors, well... we could buy a lot of bratwurst."
- "the five programs listed below are the only ones we found that work reliably for small meat processors":
  1. **Tax Increment Financing (TIF):** local governments provide loans, grants, or infrastructure up to expected increased tax revenue over 10-20 years. It is "entirely at the discretion of, local city councils and mayors." "Some plants have received tens of thousands of dollars through TIF."
  2. **Tax abatement:** also "entirely at the discretion of the local council members or county supervisors... if you don't ask, you won't get anything."
  3. **REDLG ("Red Leg"):** "in essence a zero percent interest loan for 10 years," accessed only through a local rural electric or telephone co-op. "The maximum loan amount is presently $750,000." "Successful applicants typically only finance between 5 and 17 percent of a project with this type of loan and never more than 50 percent." Principal can be deferred "up to two years for a new business." The co-op can charge up to 1% per year.
  4. **REAP:** "This program only applies to existing plants... grants can only cover up to 25% of the cost of the eligible portions of renovation. For renovations over $200,000, a feasibility study is required... can cost up to $30,000... $50,000 appears to be a realistic grant cap for this program." Tip: applying for grant plus loan guarantee lets the decision be made locally and faster.
  5. **SBA 504 loans** (CDC program).
- Royalty financing is not recommended for thin margins: "Unless your business has a high gross profit margin (say, 25% or more), you probably can't afford this."

## Caveats

- The page is undated. Its REAP limits (25%, $50k realistic cap) are **likely superseded**. REAP has a new rule effective Oct 16, 2026 (see `2026-10-02-r2-fedgrant-reap-2026-final-rule.md`).
- The REDLG $750,000 maximum may be outdated (unverified against current RD limits).

## Fit for this business [inferred]

- Three of the five "programs that work" are loans. The two grant-like items are local and political. That supports a capital plan built on debt plus owner equity, with grants treated as upside only.
- Local ask: Frederick County / Winchester economic development for TIF, abatement, or infrastructure help. Only the county can say yes; this is not verified for Frederick County.
- REDLG route: Rappahannock Electric Cooperative or Shenandoah Valley Electric Cooperative may serve the area (unverified which co-op serves the site).
