# VR&E Chapter 31 Self-Employment: what VA can and cannot buy

Sources (fetched 2026-10-12; eCFR itself was blocked by a federalregister.gov bot wall, so Cornell LII mirror used):
- 38 CFR 21.257 Self-employment: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/21.257
- 38 CFR 21.214 Furnishing supplies for special programs: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/21.214
- 38 CFR 21.258 Cost limitations on approval of self-employment plans: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/21.258
- 38 USC 3104(a)(12): https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/3104
- VA program page: https://www.va.gov/careers-employment/vocational-rehabilitation/programs/self-employment/

## Gate: eligibility (va.gov)
All must apply:
- Service member or Veteran with a **service-connected disability**
- Has an employment barrier/handicap
- Enrolled in VR&E
- Disability impairs ability to "prepare for, obtain, and maintain suitable employment"

Being a veteran alone is NOT enough. No disability rating = no Chapter 31.

What va.gov advertises: "Coordination services and help with developing a proposed business plan", business concept analysis, "Training in small-business operations, marketing, and finances", "Guidance in getting the right resources to implement your business plan". "After you develop your business plan, we'll review it and evaluate whether self-employment and the proposed business are viable options." The page gives no dollar figures.

## Statute: 38 USC 3104(a)(12)
"Such license fees and essential equipment, supplies, and minimum stocks of materials as the Secretary determines to be necessary for a veteran to begin self-employment..." Priority to "veterans with the most severe service-connected disabilities who require homebound training or self-employment". Secretary must set "criteria and cost limitations" by regulation.

## Two tiers (38 CFR 21.257(c)-(e))
- (c): VA "may provide the self-employment services listed in paragraph (d)" to any participant pursuing self-employment, and "the more extensive services listed in paragraph (e)" to "individuals with the most severe service-connected disability(ies) who require self-employment."
- (b): "most severe" = limitations "so severe as to necessitate selection of self-employment as the only reasonably feasible vocational goal."
- (d) Basic tier: "Vocational training"; "Incidental training in the management of a business"; "License or other fees required for self-employment"; "Necessary tools and supplies for the occupation"; services per 21.252.
- (e) Enhanced tier: (d) plus "the assistance described in § 21.214."

## 38 CFR 21.214(e)-(f): equipment and stock (enhanced tier)
Permitted, "limited to beginning operations":
- "Minimum stocks of materials, e.g., inventory of saleable merchandise or goods, expendable items required for day-to-day operations"
- "Essential equipment, including machinery, occupational fixtures, accessories, and appliances"
- "Other related assistance such as business license fees"

Prohibited (21.214(f)):
- "Purchase of, or part payment for, land and buildings"
- "Making full or part payment of leases or rentals"
- "Purchase or rentals of trucks, cars, or other means of transportation"
- "Stocking a farm for animal husbandry operations"

## Approval thresholds (38 CFR 21.258, full text)
"A self-employment plan with an estimated or actual cost of less than $25,000 may be approved by the Veteran Readiness and Employment (VR&E) Officer with jurisdiction. Any self-employment plan with an estimated or actual cost of $25,000 or more must be approved by the Executive Director, VR&E Service."
There is no hard regulatory dollar cap; the threshold only moves approval to VA Central Office.

## Feasibility review (21.257(f))
VA must do a comprehensive review before approving: economic viability, cost analysis specifying assistance amounts, market analysis, availability of non-VA financing, **coordination with SBA**, business site location and cost, and a training plan.

## Fit notes [inferred]
- Owner must have a VA disability rating plus an employment handicap; unknown for this owner.
- Basic tier (tools, supplies, license fees, training, e.g. a butchery course) is the realistic ask.
- Enhanced tier could cover cut-room machinery (bandsaw, grinder, vacuum packer, scales, rail/hooks, cooler refrigeration units as "fixtures"?), but only if VA rates the owner "most severely disabled".
- A mobile slaughter trailer or truck-mounted rig very likely falls under "trucks, cars, or other means of transportation" and is barred; the fixed cooler building falls under "land and buildings". Whether a towed trailer counts as transportation is an interpretation question for the VR&E Officer; ask in writing.
- Any plan over $25k goes to Central Office; expect slow approval and pressure to use SBA/non-VA financing first.
