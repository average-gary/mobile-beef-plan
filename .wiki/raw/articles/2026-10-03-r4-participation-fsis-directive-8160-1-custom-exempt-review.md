# FSIS Directive 8160.1 Rev. 1, Custom Exempt Review Process

Fetched from the Wayback snapshot of 2023-11-29 (`web.archive.org/web/2023id_/...`). Later snapshots from 2024-2025 captured only FSIS 403 pages. Issue date: **April 25, 2022**. Series 8000 (Compliance Evaluation & Enforcement). It cancels Directive 8160.1 of 9/28/2020 and FSIS Notice 67-20 (12/16/2020). The full PDF sits at `/sites/default/files/media_file/2021-02/8160.1.pdf` and was not fetched.

## What it states
- **Purpose:** instructions for OIEA Compliance Investigators (CIs), who review custom exempt facilities that are not at official establishments, and for in-plant IPP, who review custom work done inside official establishments.
- **Statutory basis (Background):** FMIA 21 U.S.C. 623(a) and PPIA 21 U.S.C. 464(c)(1)(B). "The slaughtering or preparation of an owner's animal exclusively for use in the household of such owner, by him and members of his household and his nonpaying guests and employees, is exempt from Federal inspection."
- Adulteration and misbranding provisions still apply to exempt product (21 U.S.C. 623(d); 9 CFR 303.1(f)).
- Sanitation: 9 CFR 416.1-416.6 apply to custom livestock facilities, except 416.2(g)(2)-(6). All of part 416 applies if custom work happens inside a Federal establishment.
- SRMs make product adulterated, so custom product cannot contain them. Non-ambulatory disabled cattle delivered by the owner are not eligible. Cattle that go down *after* delivery may be slaughtered if nothing else makes them unfit (Docket FSIS-2008-0022).
- Product must be marked "Not for Sale" immediately (21 U.S.C. 623(a); 9 CFR 316.16). HMSA applies to custom livestock slaughter.
- Records (21 U.S.C. 642; 9 CFR 303.1(b)(3), part 320): "such records as will fully and correctly disclose all transactions involved in their custom exempt business." Records are kept on site for 2 years after Dec 31 of the year they were made (9 CFR 320.3).
- **Review frequency:** "generally at a frequency of once-per-year." Extra reviews depend on findings, LOWs, NOWs, NOIs and consent agreements.
- **Enforcement ladder:** Letter of Warning (LOW); Notice of Warning (NOW) "for violations of statutory or regulatory requirements (e.g., sale of custom exempt product, misbranding, or noncompliance with recordkeeping requirements)"; Notice of Ineligibility (NOI) for "serious or repeated noncompliance"; consent agreements and decrees.
- **States:** States with "at least equal to" MPI programs run custom exempt reviews themselves, and FSIS audits them (Directives 5720.2/5720.3). Virginia runs its own MPI program [stated elsewhere in the wiki: VDACS OMPS].
- **Voluntary records FSIS recommends:** cattle age (under 30 months vs 30 months and older), that cattle were ambulatory "at the time they were farm-dressed or delivered to slaughter," and SRM disposal.
- Marking review asks: "Are field-dressed or farm-dressed carcasses or parts clearly marked 'Not for Sale' upon entering the facility?"

## What it does NOT say (relevant to the advising/teaching question)
- It never mentions 9 CFR 303.1(a)(1), the personal-use or own-raising exemption. That exemption sits outside the review program entirely, so FSIS has no routine review of owner slaughter.
- It never defines "operator," "participation," instructors, teachers, consultants or hired helpers.
- "Employees" shows up only as people who may *eat* the product (nonpaying guests and employees). That sets who may eat the meat, not who may do the work.

## Implications [inferred]
- [inferred] The federal review machinery attaches to a *custom exempt operation or facility*. A business that charges for slaughter or processing work on someone else's animal looks like a custom operator and falls inside this review program. A pure teacher who never handles the carcass has no category here.
- [inferred] Because the directive is silent, any line between instructing and performing has to come from the statute's text ("slaughtering by any individual of livestock of his own raising... by him") and from state guidance such as VDACS's "if the seller participates" rule. FSIS review practice does not supply it.
- [inferred] If a Virginia instructor's on-farm activity is treated as custom work, the practical exposure is a VDACS/OMPS review and possibly a NOW-equivalent for operating without a Custom Permit. The permit is free (see the VDACS custom guide), so the cheap safe harbor is to hold one anyway.
