# Penn State Extension Butcher Apprenticeship Program

**Status:** "The 2026 program is at capacity. Please fill out the link below if you are interested in the 2027 program." This shows demand outstrips supply.

## Structure (from the page and FAQ)

- "Apprentices in the program will study at the University Park campus for 3 weeks to learn the basics of butchering and are then hired into full-time positions at meat processing facilities in Pennsylvania."
- "As an apprentice, you commit to 216 hours of related technical instruction (RTI) ... and 3,000 hours of on-the-job training (OJT) ... about two years to complete."
- Session 1: "July 13 to August 7, 2026". Further "1 or 2-day short course sessions throughout the 2-year apprenticeship."
- "Before starting the program, the student must find a meat processor to host them". "Assistance will be given if needed."
- "Up to 20-30 students will be selected." Requirements: high school diploma or GED, age 18 or older. "housing is not provided."
- Format: "in-classroom instruction as well as hands-on learning opportunities in the Meats Lab. Students will harvest live animals, cut fresh beef, pork, and lamb, as well as tour operating meat processing facilities." The animals belong to the university [inferred].

## Cost

"There is a cost associated with the program, but Penn State is committed to helping attendees find funding ... Apprentices must work with their local Workforce Development Board to receive funding for the training. There is no cost to the student." "Free to apply." No tuition figure is published.

## Curriculum (144 classroom theory hours, as listed)

Orientation "boot camp" and GMPs; worker safety (knives, sharpening, PPE, OSHA); animal welfare and handling (loading, pens, chutes, stunning: mechanical, electrical, CO2); meat science (muscle to meat, comparative anatomy of beef, pork, and lamb, IMPS, grading, carcass fabrication); food safety (SOPs/SSOPs, hygiene, **ServSafe Manager certification, Basic HACCP for Meat & Poultry certification, Preventive Controls for Human Foods certification**, food defense, USDA-FSIS and FDA regulation); "The Costs of Doing Business" (COGS, a pricing exercise, "Profitability and Feasibility of Small Processors"); curing, sausage, thermal processing and smoking; packaging; poultry harvest; plant tours; and an employer-linked "Plant Project".

## Relevance [inferred]

- The nearest structured *workforce* pipeline to the Shenandoah Valley is in Pennsylvania. It trains employees for processors, not livestock owners who want to do their own slaughter, and is funded by WIOA/workforce boards rather than tuition. That leaves room in Virginia for *owner-education* offerings: short and hands-on on the owner's animal.
- A Virginia operator could serve as a host employer for 3,000-hour OJT apprentices only if a similar Virginia-registered apprenticeship exists. The PA program places apprentices only in Pennsylvania facilities (open question).
- The curriculum list is a usable syllabus outline for any paid owner or renter course (handling, stunning, carcass fabrication, food safety, costing).
