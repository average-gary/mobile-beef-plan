# Minnesota Stat. § 31A.15: Exemptions

Fetched from revisor.mn.gov. History: 1969 c 225 s 15; amended 1983, 1986, 1988, 1999, 2000, and 2020 c 89 art 4 s 24. Minnesota is a comparison state with its own "equal to" MPI program.

## Stated text
- **Subd. 1(1):** inspection does not apply "to the processing by a person of the person's own animals and the owner's preparation and transportation in intrastate commerce of the carcasses ... exclusively for use by the owner and members of the owner's household, nonpaying guests, and employees."
- **Subd. 1(2):** nor "to the custom processing by a person of animals, wild game, or fowl delivered by the owner for processing ... exclusively for use in the household of the owner by the owner and members of the owner's household, nonpaying guests, and employees."
  - Custom meat "must be identified and handled as required by the commissioner" through every phase.
  - "The custom processor may not engage in the business of buying or selling carcasses ... usable as human food unless ... inspected and passed."
- **Subd. 2:** custom processing must meet sanitary conditions set by rule.
- **Subd. 3:** adulteration and misbranding provisions (other than the inspection legend) still apply to exempt articles.

## Comparison with federal and Virginia text
- Federal 9 CFR 303.1(a)(1): "slaughtering by any individual of livestock **of his own raising**, and the preparation **by him**." Minnesota drops "own raising" and says "the person's own animals." In Minnesota, a buyer of a live animal who processes it personally fits the personal exemption on the face of the statute.
- The custom exemption in both texts is framed as processing "by a person" of animals "delivered by the owner," meaning someone *other than the owner* doing the work.
- Neither text mentions instruction, supervision or compensation.

## Relevance [inferred]
- [inferred] Under federal, Virginia, Vermont, Maine and Minnesota wording, the trigger is a non-owner *doing* the slaughter or processing. Compensation is never listed as an element. An unpaid friend who makes the cuts is, on a literal reading, doing custom processing "by a person" of an owner-delivered animal. A paid instructor who only talks is not obviously doing it. No source found states this directly.
- [inferred] Note the federal "own raising" gap. If a Virginia customer buys a steer and slaughters it themselves, 9 CFR 303.1(a)(1) literally does not fit, because the animal was not of the buyer's own raising. VDACS's guide nonetheless treats a buyer using the farm's facilities as personal use. Virginia practice appears more generous than the federal text, so get VDACS's position in writing.
