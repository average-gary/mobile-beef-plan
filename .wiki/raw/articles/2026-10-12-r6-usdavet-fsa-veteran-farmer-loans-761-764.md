# FSA Farm Loan Programs: veteran treatment (7 CFR parts 761, 762, 764)

Retrieval: the eCFR versioner API returned full parts 761, 762, and 764 as of 2026-10-01. The amendment history in part 764 includes "91 FR 56754/56755, Sept. 4, 2026", a recent rule touching §§ 764.103 and 764.201; its content was not reviewed. The fsa.usda.gov program pages loaded; the rates page timed out, so **current dollar interest rates are not verified here**.

## Definitions (§ 761.2)

- **Veteran farmer**: "a farmer who has served in the Armed Forces (as defined in 38 U.S.C. 101(10)) and who— (i) Has not operated a farm; (ii) Has operated a farm for not more than 10 years; or (iii) Is a veteran who served in the active military, naval, or air service, and who was discharged or released from that service under conditions other than dishonorable and who first obtained status as a veteran during the most recent 10-year period."
- **Veteran** (used for the funding-preference tie-breaker): "any person who served in the military, naval, or air service during any war as defined in section 101(12) of title 38". This is a narrower wartime-service test.
- **Microloan**: "a type of OL or FO of $50,000 or less made using a reduced loan application."
- **Non-eligible enterprise** includes a business that "(iii) Primarily markets goods or provides services which might be agriculturally related, but are not produced by the farming operation; or (iv) Processes or markets farm products when the majority of the commodities processed or marketed are not produced by the farming operation."

## Veteran-specific loan terms

| Item | Rule | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| ML-OL interest rate, beginning or veteran farmer | "the lower of the regular direct OL interest rate in effect at the time of loan approval or loan closing, or 5 percent" | § 764.254(a)(4) |
| OL term limit | 7 calendar years of OL closings, but "Microloans made to a beginning farmer or a veteran farmer are not counted toward this limitation" | § 764.252(e) |
| Down Payment loan | Open only to beginning, socially disadvantaged, or veteran farmers. Buyer puts 5% down. FSA lends at most 45% of the lesser of price, appraisal, or **$667,000**. A microloan used as a down payment is capped at $50,000. Total financing is capped at 95%. | §§ 764.201-.203 |
| Down Payment rate and term | "regular direct FO rate minus 4 percent, but in no case less than 1.5 percent", repaid over 20 years | § 764.204 |
| Guarantee fee waiver | No fee on "Loans to beginning or veteran farmers involved in the direct Down Payment Loan Program" | part 762 |
| Funding tie-breaker (direct) | Among applications received the same day: "First, an applicant who is a veteran of any war" | § 764.54(b) |
| Funding tie-breaker (guaranteed) | Same-day applications: "(1) An application from a veteran" | part 762 |
| FO experience | Of the 3-in-10 years of farm-management experience required, up to 2 years can be replaced by items including "(vi) Honorable discharge from the armed forces of the United States" | § 764.152(d)(1) |

## Loan limits (current eCFR / FSA pages)

- Microloans: up to $50,000 per loan (ML-OL or ML-FO). fsa.usda.gov's operating microloan page also says "up to $50,000 per loan", with terms of 1 to 7 years.
- Direct OL: $400,000. Guaranteed OL: $2,558,000, per fsa.usda.gov "Farm Operating Loans" (inflation-adjusted).
- Direct FO: $600,000. Guaranteed FO: $2,558,000, per fsa.usda.gov.
- Unverified: whether 2025-2026 legislation changed these statutory limits. eCFR § 761.8 still reads $600,000 / $400,000, and FSA's live pages match.

## Allowed OL uses (§ 764.251(a)), relevant to a rig

- "(2) Purchase of livestock, including poultry, farm equipment or fixtures, quotas and bases, and cooperative stock for credit, production, processing or marketing purposes".
- Every OL is subject to § 764.102(f): "Loan funds will not be used to establish or support a non-eligible enterprise, even if the non-eligible enterprise contributes to the farm."
- Conservation Loans are expressly exempted from 764.102(f) (§ 764.233(a): "except § 764.102(f), which does not apply to applicants for the CL Program").

## Fit for this business [inferred]

- **No veteran carve-out from the non-eligible-enterprise rule.** A veteran-owned custom kill-and-cut service for other farmers' cattle is still ineligible for FSA OL, ML, or FO money.
- **Narrow own-herd path.** A veteran farmer whose rig mostly processes their own cattle could request an ML-OL (up to $50k at 5% or less) or a direct OL (up to $400k; equipment terms of 1-7 years per FSA) for farm processing equipment. This fits the $72k-$115k kill-only trailer better than the $300k rig. The custom-exempt problem remains: meat from custom-exempt slaughter cannot be sold. The farmer would have to sell animals live, in shares, before slaughter. Whether FSA counts those animals as "produced by the farming operation" is a question for the local FSA farm loan officer. It is not answered in regulation.
- **A cleaner veteran FSA use is the land and cooler site.** A veteran farmer can use a Down Payment loan (FO minus 4%, floor 1.5%) to buy a farm that also hosts the hanging cooler. Any cooler capacity serving third parties is the non-eligible enterprise, though. Under § 761.2, a "Farm" may include facilities used in a non-eligible enterprise, but loan funds can't support that enterprise.
