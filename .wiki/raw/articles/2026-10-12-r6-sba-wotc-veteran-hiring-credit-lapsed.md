# WOTC veteran categories and status

Sources (fetched 2026-10-12):
- IRS WOTC page (Page Last Reviewed or Updated: 20-Jul-2026): https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/work-opportunity-tax-credit
- 26 USC 51 (Cornell LII): https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/51
- DOL ETA WOTC page (https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/wotc) returned almost no text to the fetcher; not used.

## Status: lapsed for hires after 12/31/2025
- IRS: "The Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2021 (Section 113 of Division EE P.L. 116-260) authorized the extension of the WOTC until December 31, 2025." "The WOTC is available for wages paid to certain individuals who begin work on or before December 31, 2025."
- 26 USC 51(c)(4): "The term 'wages' shall not include any amount paid or incurred to an individual who begins work for the employer after December 31, 2025."
- Amendment history shows repeated short lapses later fixed retroactively (2013 to 2014 to 2019 to 2020 to 2025). A 2026 extender is possible but was not found. Unverified whether any 2026 bill extended it; Cornell and IRS both still show 12/31/2025.

## Credit mechanics (IRS)
- "In general, the WOTC is equal to 40% of up to $6,000 of wages paid... the maximum tax credit is generally $2,400. A 25% rate applies to wages for individuals who perform fewer than 400 but at least 120 hours of service for the employer. Up to $24,000 in wages may be taken into account in determining the WOTC for certain qualified veterans."
- No credit for rehires. Unused credit carries back 1 year and forward 20.
- Employer must submit Form 8850 to the designated local agency (Virginia Employment Commission for VA) within "28 calendar days from the new employee's start date"; the credit is claimed on Form 5884.

## Veteran wage caps (26 USC 51(b)(3))
"$6,000 per year ($12,000 per year in the case of any individual who is a qualified veteran by reason of subsection (d)(3)(A)(ii)(I), $14,000 per year in the case of any individual who is a qualified veteran by reason of subsection (d)(3)(A)(iv), and $24,000 per year in the case of any individual who is a qualified veteran by reason of subsection (d)(3)(A)(ii)(II))."

| Qualified veteran category (IRS wording) | Wage cap | Max credit at 40% |
|---|---|---|
| Family received SNAP 3+ months of the 15 months before hire | $6,000 | $2,400 |
| Unemployed 4 weeks to under 6 months in prior year | $6,000 | $2,400 |
| Entitled to service-connected disability comp, hired within 1 year of discharge | $12,000 | $4,800 |
| Unemployed 6+ months in prior year | $14,000 | $5,600 |
| Service-connected disability AND unemployed 6+ months | $24,000 | $9,600 |

(The $ credit column is computed as 40% of the cap. Pairing of the IRS category wording to statute subclauses is inferred from the IRS list order and statute structure.)

## Interaction with OJT / apprenticeship funding (26 USC 51(c)(2)(A))
"The term 'wages' shall not include any amounts paid or incurred by an employer for any period to any individual for whom the employer receives federally funded payments for on-the-job training of such individual for such period." [inferred] GI Bill OJT pays the veteran a housing stipend, not the employer, so it likely doesn't trigger this. VR&E Special Employer Incentive (SEI) payments do go to the employer and likely would.

## Fit notes [inferred]
- The credit applies to employees, not to the owner. If the veteran is the owner, WOTC doesn't help him; it helps only if he hires veteran helpers or cutters.
- At 1-3 seasonal or part-time employees, even a renewed WOTC is a few thousand dollars a year: a nice-to-have, not a financing source.
- If renewed, the 28-day 8850 filing is the step people miss; build it into onboarding.
