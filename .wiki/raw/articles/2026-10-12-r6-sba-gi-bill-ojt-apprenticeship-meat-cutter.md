# GI Bill OJT / apprenticeship for a meat-cutter trainee

Sources (fetched 2026-10-12):
- 38 USC 3313 (Post-9/11 payment for OJT): https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/3313
- 38 USC 3677 (approval of OJT programs): https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/3677
- 38 CFR 21.4262 (other OJT courses): https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/21.4262
- VA page: https://www.va.gov/education/about-gi-bill-benefits/how-to-use-benefits/on-the-job-training-apprenticeships/
- Blocked or 404: Virginia DVS State Approving Agency page (403); benefits.va.gov OJT pages (404 after VA site migration).

## Who can use it (va.gov)
"Veterans," "Reservists," and Fry Scholarship / DEA dependents. Active-duty members are excluded. Participants "need to enter into a training contract for a set amount of time with an employer or union" and receive employer salary that increases as skills develop. Examples VA lists are trades (plumbing, electrical, law enforcement, firefighting); butchery isn't named, but nothing excludes it.

## Post-9/11 payment schedule (38 USC 3313(g)(3)(B))
Paid to the veteran, not the employer:
- Months 1-6: "the monthly amount of the basic allowance for housing payable under section 403 of title 37 for a member with dependents in pay grade E–5" (for the employer's ZIP)
- Months 7-12: "80 percent"
- Months 13-18: "60 percent"
- Months 19-24: "40 percent"
- Beyond 24 months: "20 percent"
- Plus "a monthly stipend in an amount equal to $83 for each month... for books, supplies, equipment, and other educational costs."
The 2026 E-5 with-dependents BAH for Winchester, VA (22601/22602/22603) was NOT fetched; check the DoD BAH calculator. Unverified ballpark: about $1,800-$2,300/month.

Montgomery GI Bill (Ch. 30) and other chapters pay a different flat OJT rate (75/55/35% of the full-time rate); not fetched.

## Program approval criteria (38 USC 3677; mirrored in 38 CFR 21.4262)
- Wages: "(i) upon entrance into training, are not less than wages paid nonveterans in the same training position and are at least 50 per centum of the wages paid for the job for which the veteran or person is to be trained, and (ii) such wages will be increased in regular periodic increments until, not later than the last full month of the training period, they will be at least 85 per centum of the wages paid for the job".
- Length: "The job customarily requires full-time training for a period of not less than six months and not more than two years." (Registered apprenticeships under 38 CFR 21.4261 can run longer; not fetched.)
- Records: "Adequate records are kept to show the progress made by each eligible veteran or person toward such veteran's or person's job objective."
- Approval is by the State Approving Agency (in Virginia, part of the Dept. of Veterans Services). A registered apprenticeship with Virginia's Dept. of Workforce Development and Advancement or DOL can also be approved. See existing wiki source on the RAPPCE butchery program and the Penn State butcher apprenticeship.

## Owner-in-training question
- Neither 38 USC 3677 nor 38 CFR 21.4262 as fetched mentions self-employment or owner-trainees.
- Unverified: VA/SAA practice generally treats self-employment as not approvable OJT, since there is no bona fide employer-employee relationship or wage progression. An owner who wants VA-funded training should instead use (a) a GI Bill-approved non-college degree or butchery course at an outside school, (b) GI Bill "Entrepreneurship Training" (approved SBDC/VBOC courses; listed on the VA site menu, not fetched), or (c) a paid OJT slot at another processor before launch.

## Fit notes [inferred]
- For hiring: a veteran cutter in a 12-18 month OJT gets roughly $15k-$30k of VA stipends over the program on top of wages, a strong recruiting edge for a rural custom processor that can't match packing-plant pay.
- Admin: written training plan (hours per skill: breaking, fabrication, sanitation, HACCP basics), wage schedule anchored to a journeyman cutter wage (see BLS OEWS butcher wage source), monthly hours certification to VA.
- WOTC interplay: GI Bill OJT money goes to the veteran, so it probably doesn't trigger the IRC 51(c)(2) OJT-payment exclusion (see the WOTC source); WOTC itself has lapsed for 2026 hires.
- Seasonality risk: OJT expects full-time, continuous training. A seasonal kill schedule (fall-heavy) may not support approval unless off-season cut, cure, and sausage work fills the hours.
