# Maine 22 M.R.S. § 2517-D: Rental of a licensed slaughterhouse

Source: Maine Revised Statutes, Title 22, Chapter 562-A (data extracted 1/05/2026). Enacted PL 2013 c. 252 § 5; amended PL 2021 c. 64 § 24. Maine is a comparison state, not Virginia law, and the provision covers **poultry only**.

## Stated text
- **§ 2517-D(1) Contract slaughtering:** "A commercial slaughterhouse licensed under section 2514 or a custom slaughterhouse registered under section 2514-A, but not engaging in the custom slaughter of poultry, may enter into a contract with a poultry producer who otherwise meets the requirements of the exemption for poultry producers that slaughter or process 20,000 or fewer poultry under the Poultry Products Inspection Act, 21 United States Code, Section 464(c)(3) to rent that slaughterhouse to the poultry producer for the slaughter and processing of the poultry producer's poultry. **Poultry slaughtered and processed under the rental contract must be slaughtered and processed by the poultry producer.**"
- A producer who wants to rent a slaughterhouse "that is currently used by another poultry producer that meets the requirements of the exemption, must obtain approval from the commissioner in accordance with the requirements of the federal Food Safety and Inspection Service Administrator."
- **(2)** Rental product "may not be offered for sale or transportation in interstate commerce."
- **(3)** Packaging must show the renting producer's name and address and "Exempted P.L. 90-492."
- Context from § 2514(1): Maine licenses anyone "in the business of ... preparing, processing ... or otherwise handling meat" in intrastate commerce. Categories include commercial slaughterers, processors and, since 2013, "Mobile poultry processing unit operators." Custom slaughterhouses register under § 2514-A.

## Relevance to advise, teach or rent models
- Stated: when Maine authorizes renting facilities, it attaches the exemption to **who performs the work**, which must be the renter or producer, and not to who owns the facility.
- Stated: the facility owner must *not* be doing custom poultry slaughter at the same time. Maine keeps "renting a facility" and "providing a custom service" as separate legal postures.
- Stated: no equivalent rental provision for livestock (cattle, swine, sheep, goats) appears in Ch. 562-A's section list.
- [inferred] This parallels Vermont § 3311a (buyer performs, seller does "not assist") and VDACS's "if the seller participates" rule. Across these states the consistent test is *who performs the slaughter or processing*. Payment does not appear as a test in any of them.
- [inferred] For a Virginia "rent the rig or trailer + teach" model, Maine shows a legislature treating renter-performs rental as distinct from custom service. The federal PPIA 20,000-bird exemption it rests on has no red-meat equivalent in the FMIA. For beef, the only analog is 9 CFR 303.1(a)(1), which requires the owner to slaughter livestock "of his own raising... by him." So a beef renter-performs model works cleanly only for owner-raised cattle, and only if the renter actually does the work.
