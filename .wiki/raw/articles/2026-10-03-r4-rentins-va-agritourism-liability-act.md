# Va. Code Title 3.2, Chapter 64 — Agritourism Activity Liability

Text current as displayed on LIS on 2026-10-03.

## § 3.2-6400 Definitions (2006 c.710; 2008 c.860; 2020 c.411)

- **"Agritourism activity"**: "any activity carried out on a farm or ranch that allows members of the general public, for recreational, entertainment, or educational purposes, to view or enjoy rural activities, including farming, wineries, ranching, horseback riding, historical, cultural, harvest-your-own activities, or natural activities and attractions. An activity is an agritourism activity whether or not the participant paid to participate in the activity."
- **"Agritourism professional"**: "any person who is engaged in the business of providing one or more agritourism activities, whether or not for compensation."
- **"Farm or ranch"**: "one or more areas of land used for the production, cultivation, growing, harvesting or processing of agricultural products." "Agricultural products" includes livestock and poultry.
- **"Inherent risks of agritourism activity"**: dangers "integral" to the activity, including surface and subsurface conditions; natural conditions of land, vegetation and water; "the behavior of wild or domestic animals"; and "ordinary dangers of structures or equipment ordinarily used in farming and ranching operations." The definition also includes a participant's own negligence, such as "failing to follow instructions given by the agritourism professional."

## § 3.2-6401 Liability limited

- **A.** An agritourism professional "is not liable for injury to or death of a participant resulting from the inherent risks of agritourism activities, so long as the warning contained in § 3.2-6402 is posted as required." The professional "shall plead the affirmative defense of assumption of the risk."
- **B. Exceptions (no immunity):** (1) negligence or willful/wanton disregard that proximately causes injury; (2) actual or constructive knowledge of a dangerous condition of land, facilities, equipment, or an animal's dangerous propensity, without making the danger known; (3) intentional injury.
- **C.** This limitation is in addition to other limits provided by law.

## § 3.2-6402 Notice required (2016 c.166 amendment)

- **A.** Signs must be posted at the entrance and at the activity site, in black letters at least one inch high. "Every written contract entered into by an agritourism professional for the providing of professional services, instruction, or the rental of equipment to a participant, whether or not the contract involves agritourism activities on or off the location... shall contain in clearly readable print the notice."
- **B. Required text:** "WARNING" or "ATTENTION" followed by: "Under Virginia law, there is no liability for an injury to or death of a participant in an agritourism activity conducted at this agritourism location if such injury or death results from the inherent risks of the agritourism activity. Inherent risks of agritourism activities include, among others, risks of injury inherent to land, equipment, and animals, as well as the potential for you to act in a negligent manner that may contribute to your injury or death. You are assuming the risk of participating in this agritourism activity."
- **C.** If the sign and notice rules are not met, the agritourism professional cannot use the immunity.

## Application to the business-plan options [inferred]

- **On-farm educational workshops** (for example a home-butchery or carcass-breakdown class held on a working farm) probably fit "educational purposes... to view or enjoy rural activities, including farming." Animal behavior and ordinary farm-equipment dangers are listed inherent risks. Knife-handling and firearm risks are *not* listed. Whether a knife cut in a butchery class is an "inherent risk" of agritourism is untested here, so do not rely on it.
- **Equipment rental:** § 3.2-6402(A) names "rental of equipment to a participant" contracts. That suggests the legislature expected agritourism professionals to rent equipment. But the activity must be carried out "on a farm or ranch" by someone in the agritourism business. A trailer rented off-site to a farmer for their own slaughter is probably *outside* the chapter.
- **Negligence is never shielded** (§ 3.2-6401(B)(1)). That matches *Hiett* (pre-injury negligence releases are void). The chapter only blocks suits based *exclusively* on inherent risks. Insurance remains the main risk transfer.
- **Low-cost compliance step:** add the § 3.2-6402(B) text verbatim to every workshop registration form and rental agreement, and post the 1-inch-letter sign. Leaving it out forfeits the defense entirely.
- Virginia also has a separate equine-activity liability statute (Va. Code Ch. 62 of Title 3.2). It was not fetched and is not relevant unless horses are involved.
