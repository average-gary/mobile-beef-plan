# Veteran preferences in USDA Rural Development business programs

Method: full text of 7 CFR parts 4279, 4280, and 1980 subpart K was pulled from the eCFR versioner API (date 2026-10-01) and searched for "veteran". rd.usda.gov returned 403, so FY2026/27 program notices (which can add discretionary priority categories) were **not** checked.

## Business & Industry (B&I) Guaranteed Loans, 7 CFR 4279 subpart B

- § 4279.2 definition: "Veteran. For the purposes of assigning priority points, a veteran is a person who is a veteran of any war, as defined in title 38 U.S.C. 101(12)." This is a wartime-period test, not just any service.
- § 4279.166(a): "When applications on hand otherwise have equal priority, the Agency will give preference to applications for loans from qualified veterans."
- § 4279.166(b)(2)(vi), under demographics priority: "Business is owned by a qualified veteran as defined by § 4279.2 (5 points)."
- Other points in the same section this business might earn:
  - Population priority: unincorporated area or city under 25,000, 10 points.
  - Business/industry: "provides an additional market for existing local businesses" (5); "locally owned and managed" (5); "will produce a natural resource value-added product" (5).
  - "Business that processes, distributes, aggregates, stores, and/or markets locally or regionally produced agricultural food products to underserved communities in accordance with § 4279.113(y)(5)" (10).
  - Loan features: priced at or below WSJ Prime + 1.5% (5), and at or below Prime + 1% (5 more); guarantee below 60%, 50%, or 40% of project cost (5 each).
- B&I priority scoring ranks applications against each other for guarantee authority. The Agency "may establish a minimum loan priority score" for National Office reserve funding.

## REAP, 7 CFR 4280 subpart B (§ 4280.121, RES and EEI grant scoring)

- Applicants may optionally "Identify if the borrower is a veteran ... may be used by the Agency to award priority points" (§§ 4280.118, 4280.120).
- § 4280.121(h): up to 10 State Director or Administrator points. One listed basis is (h)(3)(i): "Owned by a veteran, including but not limited to individuals as sole proprietors, members, partners, stockholders, etc., of not less than 20 percent. In order to receive points, applicants must provide a statement in their applications to indicate that owners of the project have Veteran status." This was amended at 90 FR 30560, July 10, 2025.
- REAP's general veteran definition in § 4280.103 is "served in the active military, naval, or air service, and who was discharged or released therefrom under conditions other than dishonorable" (38 U.S.C. 101(2)). Any honorably discharged veteran qualifies; wartime service is not required.

## Programs with no veteran preference in regulation

- **RMAP** (7 CFR 4280 subpart D): no occurrence of "veteran". Scoring covers the microlender (MDO) qualifications.
- **RBDG** (7 CFR 4280 subpart E): no occurrence of "veteran". Priority points go to leveraging, community trauma or distress, population, unemployment, income, and so on. These are community-level points, and RBDG grants go to public bodies and nonprofits, not to the business.
- **SECD** (7 CFR 1980 subpart K): "The Agency will reserve up to 15 percent of the funds made available in a fiscal year to each program identified in § 1980.1002" for projects implementing a Strategic Community Investment Plan. There is no veteran language.

## Fit for this business [inferred]

- Veteran ownership is worth 5 B&I points plus a tie-breaker, if the owner served during a statutory "period of war" (38 U.S.C. 101(12) / 101(11)). Post-1990 Gulf War-era service counts under current law.
- A ~$300k-$500k B&I guarantee through a local bank or Farm Credit is plausible but small for B&I. Whether lenders in the Winchester area will do a B&I this size, and the current guarantee fee, are open questions.
- Frederick County / Winchester urbanized-area rurality limits for B&I (non-urban, under 50,000) need checking per site. A cooler inside Winchester city limits may fail.
- For REAP, a veteran-owned (at least 20%) cooler project with rooftop solar or high-efficiency refrigeration could pick up discretionary points. REAP is already covered generally in the round-2 file.
