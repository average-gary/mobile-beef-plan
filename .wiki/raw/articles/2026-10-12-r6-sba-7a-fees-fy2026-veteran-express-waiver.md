# SBA 7(a) fees FY2026 and veteran fee relief

Primary sources (fetched 2026-10-12):
- SBA Information Notice 5000-872051, "7(a) Fees Effective October 1, 2025 for Fiscal Year 2026 and 90-Day Rule Clarification", effective Aug 28, 2025, expires 10/1/26. PDF above (retrieved via Wayback `id_` copy; notice page https://www.sba.gov/document/information-notice-5000-872051-7a-fees-effective-october-1-2025-fiscal-year-2026 now 404s on the new sba.gov).
- 15 USC 636 (Cornell LII): https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/636
- SBA "Fee Relief for Manufacturers" factsheet, 3/2026: https://www.sba.gov/sites/default/files/2026-03/Fee%20Relief%20for%20Manufacturers%20-%20Factsheet.pdf

## FY2026 7(a) fee schedule (loans approved 10/1/2025 to 9/30/2026)
- Lender annual service fee: "0.55% of the outstanding balance of the guaranteed portion of each loan... Lenders may not pass the Lender's Annual Service Fee on to the Borrower."
- Upfront fee, maturity over 12 months:
  - <= $150,000: 2% of guaranteed portion (lender may keep up to 25%)
  - $150,001 to $700,000: 3% of guaranteed portion
  - $700,001 to $5,000,000: 3.5% of guaranteed portion up to $1M, plus 3.75% over $1M
- Maturity 12 months or less: 0.25%.
- Exceptions: "Except for Export Working Capital Program (EWCP) loans, Working Capital Program (WCP) loans, and SBA Express loans made to veteran-owned businesses..."
- Manufacturers: "For loans to manufacturers (NAICS sectors 31-33) of $950,000 or less, the upfront fee is 0% (does not apply to MARC loans)."
- 90-day rule: two or more 7(a) loans to the same applicant within 90 days are combined for guaranty percent and fee. "Lenders are not permitted to split loans for the purpose of avoiding fees."

## Veteran SBA Express waiver (the live veteran benefit)
Notice: "For all SBA Express loans made to businesses owned and controlled by a veteran or spouse of a veteran, the Upfront Fee will be $0 in accordance with section 7(a)(31)(G) of the Small Business Act (15 U.S.C. § 636(a)(31)(G)). Lenders must document in their loan file the eligibility for fee relief."

Statute, 15 USC 636(a)(31)(G)(i): "The Administrator may not collect a guarantee fee described in paragraph (18) in connection with a loan made under this paragraph to a veteran or spouse of a veteran on or after October 1, 2015." No sunset or subsidy-cost exception appears in the text.
(G)(ii) eligible: a veteran (632(q)(4)); anyone eligible for the DoD Transition Assistance Program (i.e., transitioning service members); Reserve/Guard members; spouses of these; surviving spouses of those who died on active duty or of a service-connected disability.
Express max: 636(a)(31)(D) "The maximum loan amount under the Express Loan Program is $500,000." (SBA guarantee on Express is 50%; from general SBA knowledge, not re-fetched this round.)

## Veteran 7(a) fee pilot (636(a)(33)), likely dead
"The fee on a veteran participation loan shall be equal to 50 percent of the fee otherwise applicable", and the pilot "shall terminate at the end of the second full fiscal year after the date that the Administrator establishes the pilot program." The FY2026 notice has no veteran fee cut for standard 7(a), so it is not in effect.

## History (partly unverified)
- Patriot Express (2007 to Dec 31, 2013): veteran pilot loans up to $500k. Replaced by "Veterans Advantage" fee relief for SBA Express and some 7(a) loans (IN 5000-1354 renewed it; IN 5000-1955 covered fees on veteran 7(a) loans). Notice titles confirmed through Wayback CDX, but the notice text did not load. Dates and terms are from memory: unverified.
- Veterans Entrepreneurship Act of 2015 (IN 5000-1356) made the Express waiver permanent law, now 636(a)(31)(G).

## Manufacturer fee relief (factsheet, 3/2026)
"SBA is eliminating upfront guaranty fees on most 7(a) loans and all 504 loans approved between October 1, 2025–September 30, 2026 for manufacturing businesses. If your business falls under NAICS codes 31–33, you qualify." 7(a): 0% for <= $950k. 504: 0% upfront and 0% annual service fee. Example: a $950k 10-yr loan saves $24,938.

## Fit notes [inferred]
- Veteran ownership needs 51%+ "owned and controlled" by the veteran/spouse; the lender documents it (DD-214).
- Express is lender-delegated and fast, but the 50% guarantee means lenders take more risk; a startup custom-exempt processor may get pushed to standard 7(a) anyway.
- The manufacturer route (NAICS 311611) is worth raising with the lender whatever the owner's veteran status. A lender could code a mobile service business under 115210 (Support Activities for Animal Production) instead, which is outside 31-33. Check which code the lender uses.
