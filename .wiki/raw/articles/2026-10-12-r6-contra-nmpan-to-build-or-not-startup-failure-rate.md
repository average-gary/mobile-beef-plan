# Startup processing-plant failure rate (NMPAN webinar transcript)

The webinar is from 2011 and covers fixed plants, not MSUs specifically. It is the best primary data found this round on new-venture failure in small meat processing.

## Extracted facts (from transcript)

- "For many reasons, start-up processing facilities have a high failure rate." (overview)
- DeHaan's sample: "Of those 35 projects, over half of them failed to raise the money... Those under the red slice of the pie raised the money but are now shut down for a variety of reasons... That would actually be higher than the national average of start-up businesses."
- "There are five of the 35 that are running today, that are considered successful."
- Top failure theme: "#1 is they run out of operating capital and have limited opportunities to find more with investors and lenders. They can't weather the storm when the market is upside down. They run into a cash crunch."
- Example: Central Dakota Beef, Harvey, ND. It failed the first time. The second time "They ran that on very slim operating capital, the market was upside down, they just couldn't weather that very long."
- On producer ownership: "Today, seeing as many failures as I have, I'm not quite as aggressive on that front anymore... Producers produce. It's a different kind of a business."
- Raines: "some plants who are operating under USDA, they're expected to match prices of area custom-exempt plants." Another cause: "They underestimated some transportation costs... They failed to take into account the need for buying a truck." Many failures were "because it was just a person bent on letting their philosophy... trump their business sense."

## Fit for this business [inferred]

- The danger is not getting a grant. It is a grant that funds the trailer or cooler while working capital stays thin. Plan for 6 to 12 months of operating reserve that is separate from any grant match.
- Transport and truck costs are a named failure cause. This business's mobile model depends on a truck, fuel, and drive time, so cost them fully.
- Raines's price-matching point cuts the other way here: this business *is* the custom-exempt price competitor.
