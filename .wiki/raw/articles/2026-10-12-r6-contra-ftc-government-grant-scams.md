# FTC: government grant scams

## Extracted facts

- Tactics: "Scammers reach you in lots of ways. They put ads online for (fake) government grants." They impersonate real agencies or invent ones, such as the "Federal Grants Administration." They make unsolicited contact by phone, text, email, or social media and "ask you for information or money."
- Fees are the red flag: "Don't pay for a list of government grants — and don't pay any up-front fees." Real grant information is free at grants.gov.
- Payment rails scammers use include gift cards, wire transfers, cryptocurrency, and cash reload cards.
- "The government won't get in touch out of the blue about grants."
- Report to ReportFraud.ftc.gov. "The FTC uses and shares reports with law enforcement partners."
- The fetched page contains **no veteran-specific language**.

## Gaps / unverified

- Veteran-targeted grant-writing scams (paid "grant writers" who promise veteran-only business grants) were **not verified** from a primary source this round. The FTC and VA have separate veteran-scam pages; they were not fetched.
- Not covered by this page: legitimate paid grant writers. Paying a grant writer is legal. The scam pattern is a guaranteed award or an up-front fee for a non-existent program. [inferred]

## Fit for this business [inferred]
