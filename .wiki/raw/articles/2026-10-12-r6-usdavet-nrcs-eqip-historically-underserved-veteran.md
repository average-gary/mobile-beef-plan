# EQIP veteran treatment (7 CFR part 1466)

Retrieval: eCFR versioner API (part 1466, as of 2026-10-01). nrcs.usda.gov did not respond to curl in this session, so the Virginia FY2026/27 EQIP payment schedule and ranking dates were **not** verified.

## Definitions (§ 1466.3)

- "Historically underserved producer means a person, joint operation, legal entity, or Indian Tribe who is a beginning farmer or rancher, socially disadvantaged farmer or rancher, limited resource farmer or rancher, or veteran farmer or rancher."
- "Veteran farmer or rancher means a producer who meets the definition in section 2501(a) of the Food, Agriculture, Conservation, and Trade Act of 1990, as amended (7 U.S.C. 2279(a))." That statute requires Armed Forces service AND (never operated a farm, OR operated 10 years or less, OR first became a veteran in the most recent 10 years).

## Money terms for HUPs (including veterans)

- **Higher rate**: "a participant that meets the definition of a historically underserved producer under § 1466.3 may be awarded the applicable payment rate and an additional rate that is not less than 25 percent above the applicable rate, provided this increase does not exceed 90 percent of the estimated costs incurred for implementing the conservation practice."
- **Advance payment**: "with respect to participants who are historically underserved producers, NRCS may issue advance payments of at least 50 percent and not to exceed 100 percent of the anticipated amount of the costs incurred for the purpose of purchasing materials or services to implement a conservation practice." This depends on an "NRCS-approved practice design prior to approval of the advance payment".
- NRCS must notify HUPs at enrollment of the advance-payment option and document their election (§ 1466.5(d)).
- NRCS commits to "special outreach" to veterans, among other groups.
- **Limits**: each contract is capped at $450,000 unless waived. The general aggregate payment limit per person or legal entity is $450,000 (the regulatory text still cites FY2019-2023; the current farm-bill period wording was not verified).
- **CIG**: NRCS "may set aside up to 10 percent of the total funds available for CIG" for HUP applicants or organizations serving them.
- farmers.gov's veteran page (separate file) adds: EQIP and CSP give veterans "ranking priority" and refer to "5% of funds set aside for beginning/disadvantaged producers". The statutory set-aside is for beginning and socially disadvantaged producers. Veterans get preference *within* it under the statute (16 U.S.C. 3839aa-2, **not fetched in this round, unverified**).

## Fit for this business [inferred]

- EQIP is for **agricultural producers on eligible land**, not for a processing business. The mobile rig and fixed cooler are not conservation practices.
- If the veteran owner also runs a cow-calf or grass-finishing operation in Frederick County, the farm itself could use HUP terms for practices like an animal mortality facility, composting facility, waste storage, heavy-use area protection, or fencing and watering. On a $40k practice, the difference between standard and HUP rates plus a 50%+ advance can be several thousand dollars and solves the cash-flow problem. NRCS practice codes and Virginia rates were not verified here.
- Composting slaughter offal from *other farmers'* animals on the owner's farm is a commercial waste activity, and also a VA DEQ solid-waste question (see the existing round-2 disposal files). Treat EQIP eligibility for an offal-compost pad as **unlikely** without written confirmation from the Virginia NRCS State Conservationist's office.
