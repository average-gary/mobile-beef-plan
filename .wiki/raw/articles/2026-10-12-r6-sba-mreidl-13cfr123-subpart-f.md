# MREIDL rules (13 CFR Part 123 Subpart F)

Fetched section by section from Cornell LII (2026-10-12). The old sba.gov MREIDL page URLs 404 on the redesigned sba.gov; sba.gov/veterans only says "MREIDL provides loans to help with losses" for businesses whose employees "in Reserves or National Guard" are called to duty.

## Definitions (123.500)
- "Essential employee is an individual (whether or not an owner of a small business) whose managerial or technical expertise is critical to the successful day-to-day operations of a small business."
- "Military reservist is a member of a reserve component of the Armed Forces ordered to active service." Active service = active duty or full-time National Guard duty (10 USC 101(d)(3)).
- "Substantial economic injury": the business cannot meet obligations, pay ordinary operating expenses, or provide its usual product/service. "Loss of anticipated profits or a drop in sales is not considered substantial economic injury for this purpose."

## Eligibility (123.501)
(a) small business under part 121 at call-up; (b) owner is a reservist and essential employee, or the business employs one; (c) call-up "for a period of more than 30 consecutive days"; (d) substantial economic injury from the absence; (e) owners and affiliates "have used all reasonably available funds, and you are unable to obtain credit elsewhere".

## Ineligible (123.502), selected
- (b) Business "assumed the risk", e.g. hired the essential employee after call-up orders.
- (d) "Your business is an agricultural enterprise. Agricultural enterprise means a business primarily engaged in the production of food and fiber, ranching and raising of livestock, aquaculture and all other farming and agriculture-related industries." Mixed businesses whose primary activity is non-agricultural may apply, "but loan proceeds may not be used, directly or indirectly, for the benefit of the agricultural enterprises".
- Also excluded: lending, speculation, government-owned entities, illegal activity, etc.

## Timing (123.503, amended 89 FR 91539, Nov. 20, 2024)
"any time beginning on the date the essential employee receives notice of expected call-up and ending one year after the date the essential employee is discharged or released from active service." AA/ODR&R may extend by "no more than one additional year".

## Amount (123.506-507)
Up to the economic injury "until normal operations resume... up to a maximum of $2 million." Waiver above $2M only for major employers (10% of county workforce, 250+ employees, etc.); not relevant here.

## Uses (123.508-509)
"(a) Meet obligations as they mature, (b) Pay ordinary and necessary operating expenses, or (c) Enable the business to market, produce or provide products or services ordinarily marketed... which cannot be done as a result of the essential employee's military call-up." Prohibited uses are those in 123.303(b) (general EIDL exclusions, e.g. refinancing, expansion; not re-fetched).

## Rate, disbursement, collateral
- 123.512: "4 percent per annum or less", published quarterly in the Federal Register. The current quarterly rate was not fetched.
- 123.511: disbursed only after call-up orders are provided, "in quarterly installments" based on continued need shown in financial statements.
- 123.513: no collateral generally for loans of $50,000 or less; above that, available collateral is required, but SBA "will not decline a loan if you do not have a particular amount of collateral".
- Term: not in these sections (EIDLs generally run up to 30 years; unverified for MREIDL).

## Fit notes [inferred]
- Not a startup capital source. The business must already exist, and proceeds cannot buy the rig or cooler.
- Matters only if the owner, or a key cutter whose skill is "critical", is a current Guard/Reserve member. Then it bridges payments on the rig loan during a deployment over 30 days.
- Ag-enterprise exclusion: custom slaughter is coded NAICS 311611 (manufacturing), so it is probably not "agricultural"; still, document primary activity as slaughter/processing services.
- Don't hire a reservist who already has orders and expect MREIDL cover (123.502(b)).
