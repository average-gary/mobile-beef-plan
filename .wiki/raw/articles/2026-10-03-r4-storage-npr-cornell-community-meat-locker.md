# A Carnivore's Solution To Space Constraints: The Meat Locker

NPR The Salt, Solvejg Wastvedt, 2015-09-22.

## The Corning, NY community meat locker (Cornell Cooperative Extension)

- "A group in upstate New York wants to overcome that hurdle with an old concept: They've brought back the meat locker." The hurdle: "Most people don't have enough freezer space" for a whole animal bought from a farmer.
- Location and format: "in the basement of a building near downtown. It's stocked with rows of plastic bins. Rent the large ones for $8 a month, or the small ones for $5."
- Access model (Kerri Bartlett, CCE): "we'll bring the bin out to you, and you can take out what you want from your bin, and then we put it back in. They're all labeled." [inferred: staff-controlled access, with no customer entry to the freezer]
- Precedent: CCE "previously opened a meat locker in Ithaca, N.Y., in 2014. The Ithaca meat locker has proved so popular, it now has a waiting list."
- Matt LeRoux (Cornell Cooperative Extension) pictured. CCE was "working with several [cities] that want to bring back their own versions."
- Value pitch: "By buying in bulk, you'll save over $200 buying, you know, a quarter of a beef versus buying it by the cut in the store." Renter Ray Towner: running a home fridge and freezer costs "more than $8 a month."
- Demand: crowd "surprisingly varied"; small city apartments named as a driver.
- History quote: "Meat lockers were common in the 1950s ... when home freezers became popular, meat lockers disappeared."

## Locker plant history (secondary: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locker_plant, rev. 2026-08-04; cites USDA Marketing Research Report 779 on AgEcon Search, not retrievable this round)

- 1908: Chico Ice and Cold Storage (California) offered rental locker space.
- 1928: first purpose-built locker plant, Parsons, Kansas.
- 1934: first combined processing and locker plant, Creston, Iowa.
- Peak of 11,608 plants in 1951; 10,854 in 1954 (down 1% from 1953), concentrated in Midwestern farm states.
- Decline driven by home freezers and supermarket fresh-meat counters.

## Relevance [inferred]

- The modern version is a rent-a-bin freezer for already-packaged meat. In the CCE model customers buy inspected or custom-exempt packaged cuts and the locker stores them; there is no processing.
- Regulatory status is not covered. Under Va. Code 3.2-5404(10) and the VDACS Handlers Permit guide (same round), a Virginia rent-a-bin locker storing for others is likely the "public warehouse" class (free, 3-year permit). For custom-exempt "Not for Sale" product held for its owner, no source found states the rule; confirm with VDACS OMPS.
- $5-8/month per bin (2015) is one pricing benchmark for a locker add-on.
