# Vermont 6 V.S.A. § 3311a: Livestock; inspection; licensing; personal slaughter; itinerant slaughter

Fetched from the Vermont Statutes Online, which includes 2025 session actions. History: added 2013, No. 83, § 4; amended 2015 No. 64; 2015 No. 98 (Adj. Sess.); 2019 No. 83; 2021 No. 47, § 1a. Vermont is a comparison state, not Virginia law.

## Stated text (key parts quoted)
- **(a)(1)** "'Assist in the slaughter of livestock' means the act of slaughtering or butchering an animal and shall not mean the farmer's provision of a site on the farm for slaughter, provision of implements for slaughter, or the service of disposal of the carcass or offal from slaughter."
- **(a)(2)** "Sanitary conditions" means a farm site that is clean and free of contaminants and is located or designed to prevent water pollution and adulteration.
- **(b) Owner-raised exemption** (this mirrors 9 CFR 303.1(a)(1)): no license or inspection is needed for "the slaughter by an owner of livestock that the owner raised for the owner's exclusive use or for the use of members of his or her household and his or her nonpaying guests and employees."
- **(c) Purchaser on-farm slaughter.** Exempt only if ALL of these hold:
  1. One or more persons buy livestock from the farmer who raised it.
  2. The farmer is registered with the Secretary as selling livestock for slaughter under this subsection.
  3. "The individual or individuals who purchased the livestock performs the act of slaughtering the livestock, as the owner of the livestock."
  4. Slaughter happens on the selling farm, with the farmer's approval.
  5. Conditions are sanitary.
  6. "The farmer who sold the livestock to the individual or individuals **does not assist** in the slaughter of the livestock."
  7. Annual caps: **30 swine; 10 cattle; 80 sheep or goats; or a combination of up to 12,000 lb live weight per year.**
  8. The farmer keeps a record of each slaughter and reports quarterly (Apr 15, Jul 15, Oct 15, Jan 15). If the farmer fails to report, the Secretary may suspend the farmer's authority.
  9. The buyer may halve or quarter the carcass, but "solely for the purpose of transport from the farm."
  10. Slaughter must be humane (§ 3131(6)).
- **(d)-(e) Itinerant slaughterer:** exempt from license and inspection. May slaughter "livestock owned by a person on the farm where the livestock was raised" if meat goes "only as whole, halved, or quartered carcasses to the person who owned the animal" for personal or household use or nonpaying guests, under sanitary conditions and humane slaughter.
- **(f)** Carcass and offal disposal follows Vermont's Required Agricultural Practices.

## Relevance to advising, teaching and consulting
- Stated: the line Vermont draws is **physical performance**, meaning "the act of slaughtering or butchering." Providing the site, the tools, and offal or carcass disposal is expressly *not* assisting.
- Stated: the statute does not mention instruction, teaching or verbal advice either way.
- Stated: whether the helper is paid is not the test. A paid **itinerant slaughterer** doing the work is a separate lawful category, but only for animals on the farm where they were raised and only as carcass halves or quarters.
- [inferred] Under Vermont's definition, an instructor who stands by and talks the owner through the kill and dressing, without cutting or handling, would not be "assisting." Once the instructor makes cuts, they are performing, and the exemption that requires the buyer to do the work would fail.
- [inferred] For Virginia, VDACS's rule ("If the seller participates in the slaughter or processing activity, then the facility owner is subject to the custom exempt criteria and must have a Custom Permit") is broader and undefined. Vermont's definition is a useful *persuasive* model of what "participates" might mean, but VDACS is not bound by it. Ask OMPS in writing.
- [inferred] Vermont's tool, site and disposal carve-out suggests a model of renting equipment plus instruction plus offal removal that stays outside custom licensing in Vermont. Whether Virginia agrees is unverified.
