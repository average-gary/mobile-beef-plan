# NMPAN: Mobile Poultry Processing Units — Reports from the Field (webinar, April 5, 2012)

This is a 90-minute NMPAN webinar with a transcript. Speakers: Jennifer Hashley (New Entry Sustainable Farming Project, MA), Angela Caporelli (Kentucky Dept. of Agriculture), Jefferson Munroe (Island Grown Initiative, MA), Chelsea Bardot Lewis (Vermont Agency of Agriculture), and Jan Tusick (Lake County CDC, MT). Moderator: Arion Thiboumery. Note: the transcript spells the IGI speaker "Monroe" in one place.

Relevance: these are the closest published analogue to renting processing equipment to farmers. The extraction focuses on insurance and liability allocation and on rental pricing. Note that Island Grown *Farmers Cooperative* (the red-meat unit) was already ingested in earlier rounds; this is the separate poultry-unit webinar.

## Insurance and liability allocation (direct quotes)

- **Island Grown Initiative (MA)** Q&A, "Who is providing your liability insurance?": "Just a local insurance provider. Because the equipment is rented by the farmer, our liability just covers transport of the equipment. The farmers have to get their own liability insurance to cover the product that it processed." The moderator adds: "Angela also noted that farmers are responsible for their own liability on their farm policy. Other larger markets require more insurance."
- **IGI model design:** "the farmer rent[s] the equipment from Island Grown Initiative and the farmer hires a trained crew. That helps to lower the liability costs for IGI."
- **Montana (Lake County CDC):** "The insurance was strictly to cover the unit on the road. We depended on the producers to have on-farm liability insurance to cover the costs of processing their birds."
- **Kentucky:** "We did have producers pick up the unit and bring it to their farm and run it and bring it back. However, we had some issues such as cleanliness, how they returned it, the liability insurance that we required for those farmers to take it with their own vehicle, did they have a strong enough vehicle, was it going to be taken care of well..." The unit has an aluminum floor, so some cleaning chemicals cannot be used. Kentucky moved to fixed docking stations (two operating, one planned). On ownership: "We found through legal and liability issues that it was easier to have a state entity own it" (Kentucky State University).
- **New England Small Farm Institute / New Entry (MA):** the rental fees covered "out-of-pocket costs, insurance, and some minor repairs." They did not cover depreciation, maintenance escrow, or management.
- **Damage disputes (MA, Hashley):** "things that travel with the unit don't always continue in transit and disappear from inventory... When things break, we've seen a lot of, 'It's not my fault. I didn't do it.' It's really up to the manager of the unit to figure out if this is something you can charge the users for."

## Rental pricing reported (2011-2012 dollars)

| Program | Fee structure |
|---|---|
| NESFI Unit 1 (MA, open-air) | $100/yr membership + $2.25/bird; user pays transport; manager travels with unit. 2011 net over management costs: $203; >2,000 birds/yr for 3 users. Rebuild cost ~$30,000 |
| New Entry Unit 2 (MA, enclosed) | No membership; sliding-scale rental; user pays transport; no traveling manager; >2,500 birds first season |
| MA licensing | $225 producer/processor license fee per producer; hiring an already-licensed crew avoids it |
| Kentucky (KDA/KSU) | Chickens $75/day for first 50 + $0.75/bird; turkeys $75/day for first 15 + $3.50 each; rabbits $50/day for first 50; fish $75/day; $10/hr manager beyond 8-hr shift. 2011: 13 users, ~1,500 birds, 240 rabbits, >75 facility managers trained |
| Island Grown Initiative (MA) | <30 birds $200 all-in; <50 birds $250; licensed processors <50 birds $65 rental + $4.25/bird crew fee (delivered vacuum-sealed); 50-125 birds $135 rental. Coordinator stipend $300/mo x ~8 mo; maintenance $500-$1,000/yr. Break-even ~50 processings/season; 65-75 covers replacement on a 12-yr depreciation |
| Lake County CDC (MT) | $1.75/bird to cover repairs; ~3,000 birds/yr across 4 producers |

## Takeaways for a Virginia beef/cooler-trailer rental model [inferred]

- The established allocation is: the owner carries **auto/transport liability and physical damage (inland marine) on the unit**, and the renter's **farm liability policy** covers the processing operation and the product. Applied to Virginia, the rental contract should require a certificate of insurance from the renter naming the owner as additional insured. Under *Hiett* (244 Va. 191), a renter waiver will not shield the owner from its own negligence for bodily injury.
- "Wet-rental with crew" (IGI) and "dry rental, renter tows" (early KY) are different risk profiles. KY dropped renter towing. Owner-towed delivery to docking stations reduces hitching and towing exposure.
- None of the five programs disclosed an annual premium figure. Premium benchmarks still need a broker quote.
