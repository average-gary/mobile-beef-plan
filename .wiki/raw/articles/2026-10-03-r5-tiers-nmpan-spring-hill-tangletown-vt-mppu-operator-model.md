# NMPAN profile: Spring Hill Poultry Processing / Tangletown Farm MPPU (Vermont)

Source: https://www.nichemeatprocessing.org/tangletown-farm-poultry-processing/ (NMPAN case profile, "Updated March 2018"). The prices and costs in the profile describe the unit's first season (2009) under the State of Vermont and operator George Eisenhardt (Spring Hill Poultry Processing), before 2012. The round-4 file `2026-10-03-r4-rental-nmpan-mobile-units-in-operation.md` lists Tangletown in one line only. The NC and KY *farmer-rental* MPPU programs are already ingested (r4 rental files). This profile is the **operator-run ("we-do-it")** counterpart.

## Ownership and lease structure
- The Vermont legislature appropriated **$85,000 in 2008**. The unit "ended up costing about $93,000 to build", with the extra $7,000 from the Castanea Foundation. Builder: Brothers Body and Equipment (Ohio), delivered October 2008. The state later incurred about **$7,000 more** for design changes and operational issues on the prototype.
- "It took almost a year to find someone to operate it."
- "The lease-to-purchase agreement consists of two-year renewable leases with up to four renewals and a buy-out option. The leaseholder will automatically own the unit after ten years of lease payments."
- **Lease: $9,300/yr**, "1/10th the cost to the state to build the unit". Required repairs and retooling were deducted from the lease payments.
- The operator's own capital risk was the tow vehicle and some equipment. An F-550 (about 7,400 lb) was "the minimum size vehicle required for transporting the 18,000 lb. trailer". A local agricultural bank approved his truck loan before his business plan was finished.
- "The state did not have a formal business plan for the unit."

## Pricing (first season, 2009)
- Base, per head: **$2.75/chicken; $4.75/turkey, or $6.75 if over 25 lbs.**
- Add-ons, per bird, all species: **$0.50 to bag, $0.75 to vacuum pack.**
- "Same rates for state inspected and exempt (under the per farmer max of 1,000 chickens/250 turkeys)."
- **Mileage: $0.95/mile one-way charge (2009), to be $1.05 in 2010.**
- **Minimum: 100 chickens or 50 turkeys per producer.** "He will only process smaller numbers if a producer can arrange to bring the birds to a farm site where George is processing other birds." This is an aggregation or "pooled farm call" rule.
- "(Fees will be updated per 1st season operating costs.)"

## Costs and operations
- Capacity: 250 chickens or 100 turkeys per day with two people. On distant sites the operator could leave as early as 4 AM and return at 8 or 9 PM.
- Insurance (trailer, truck, product liability): **$3,500 a year**.
- Variable costs: propane, diesel, labor, maintenance. "Four 40-pound LP tanks have lasted for >1 day of 1 person processing 100 birds."
- Fixed costs: lease ($9,300/yr), truck loan, depreciation.
- Scheduling: "George tries to schedule 2 weeks in advance". The state designated one inspector for the MPU, with backups.
- "One of the challenges to operating the unit profitably has been the absence of cost data for this type of business model."
- "George says he underestimated the demand for the MPPU and its potential to grow ... His business plan also underestimated his expenses." "Volume is where you make your money."
- He planned a grant application for a refrigerated vehicle with chill tanks and boning space, which would need an extra employee.

## Outcome (2012-2018)
- "In January 2012, this unit transitioned to new ownership. After the original operator decided not to renew his lease, the state of Vermont put the unit up for auction. It was purchased by Tangletown Farm, in Middlesex, VT."
- "As of 2018, they are utilizing the unit, but only to process their own birds ... Currently, they are unable to let other farmers use the unit due to liability risk."

## Relevance to a tiered mobile beef service [inferred]
- This is the clearest small-scale precedent for the structure **base per-head fee + per-unit packaging add-ons + one-way mileage + per-stop minimum**. Backyard Butchery (r3) uses the same structure for beef.
- The minimum-with-pooling rule (a small producer can join a site already booked) is a ready template for a beef "farm-call day" where neighbors share a stop.
- Two failure signals: the operator underpriced from missing cost data and did not renew the lease, and the next owner closed the unit to outside users over liability. Any "you-do-it" or assisted-rental tier needs insurance and waiver structure solved first (see the r4 rentins files on Virginia waiver law).
- The state-owned, operator-leased model (lease at about 10% of build cost per year, ownership after 10 years) is one more ownership option to set beside farmer self-rental (NC/KY) and private ownership.
