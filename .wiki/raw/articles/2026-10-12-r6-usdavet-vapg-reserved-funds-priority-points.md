# VAPG (7 CFR 4284 Subpart J): what veteran status actually buys

Retrieval note: www.ecfr.gov HTML redirected to a bot-block page. The text was pulled from the eCFR versioner API (`/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-01/title-7.xml?part=4284&subpart=J`), so it is the codified text as of 2026-10-01. rd.usda.gov returned 403 (Akamai), so the FY2026 VAPG notice (amounts, deadline, Administrator priority categories) was **not** verified in this round.

## Reserved funds: veterans are not a reserve category

§ 4284.916(a), verbatim:
> "(1) 10 percent of total program funding to fund Projects that benefit Beginning Farmers or Ranchers or Socially-Disadvantaged Farmers or Ranchers; and
> (2) 10 percent of total program funding to fund Projects where a majority of the requested grant amount goes to improving Food Safety for the purpose of enhancing market access; and
> (3) 10 percent of total program funding to fund Projects that propose development of Mid-Tier Value Chains."

- Reserved funds left unobligated on Sept 30 roll into the next year's general competition (§ 4284.916(b)).
- To use the Beginning Farmer reserve, the applicant "must self-certify that more than 50 percent of its owners are a Beginning Farmer or Rancher" (§ 4284.923(a)).
- Food Safety reserve: more than 50% of Project Cost must go to post-harvest food safety tied to processing or marketing a value-added product (§ 4284.923(c)).
- The farmers.gov line "Beginning farmer and rancher veterans receive priority ranking" (see the separate farmers.gov file) is consistent with this. A *beginning* veteran (10 years or less farming) can enter the 10% Beginning Farmer reserve **because they are beginning, not because they are veterans**.

## Definitions (§ 4284.902)

- **Veteran Farmer or Rancher**: "a farmer or rancher who has served in the Armed Forces, as defined in 7 U.S.C. 2279, and who either has not operated a Farm or Ranch or has operated a Farm or Ranch for not more than 10 years; or is a Veteran who first obtained status as a veteran during the most recent 10-year period."
- **Beginning Farmer or Rancher**: an Agricultural Producer (other than a Harvester) that has operated a farm or ranch for no more than 10 years, or an entity owned more than 50% by such farmers.
- **Family Farm**: owners provide the daily physical labor and management, and hired help "only supplements family labor".

## Priority points (§ 4284.924 and the scoring section)

- Veteran applicants must "provide a self-certification that they meet the definition of a Veteran Farmer or Rancher" (§ 4284.924(b)).
- The scoring criterion "Priority points (0 to 10 points)" applies in both the general and the reserved competitions:
  - "(i) 5 priority points will be awarded if the Applicant meets the requirements for one of the following categories ... Beginning Farmer or Rancher, Socially-Disadvantaged Farmer or Rancher, Veteran Farmer or Rancher, or operator of a Small- or Medium-Sized Farm or Ranch that is structured as a Family Farm, Farmer or Rancher Cooperative, or are proposing a Mid-Tier Value Chain Project. Applicants will not be awarded more than five (5) points even if they qualify for more than one of the priority categories."
  - "(ii) 5 additional priority points will be awarded if the Applicant is an Agricultural Producer Group, Farmer or Rancher Cooperative, or Majority-Controlled Producer-Based Business Venture whose Project 'best contributes to creating or increasing marketing opportunities' for operators of ... Veteran Farmers or Ranchers."
- Administrator or State Director points run 0 to 10, for geographic diversity, underserved areas, emergencies, or Department priorities. The categories are published when the application window opens (§ 4284.930). Whether FY2026 named veterans as a category was not verified.
- Funding order: reserve applications are funded first in rank order. Unfunded ones then compete for general funds down to "the minimum score for funding of 50 points". Majority-Controlled Producer-Based Business Ventures are capped at "10 percent of total grant funds expected to be obligated each funding cycle".
- Grant cap: the annual maximum is set in the yearly notice, "but in no event may the total amount of grant funds provided to a grant Recipient exceed $500,000."

## Fit for this business [inferred]

- Veteran status does not remove the core VAPG barrier already in the wiki: a custom-exempt business that slaughters *other farmers'* animals is not an Agricultural Producer adding value to its *own* commodity.
- Path A: a veteran cattle farmer applies as an Independent Producer for working capital to market *their own* beef. That earns 5 points (veteran, or beginning, not both), plus possible entry to the 10% beginning reserve if the farmer has farmed 10 years or less. This does not fund a custom-exempt rig, because custom-exempt meat cannot be sold. VAPG products require inspected processing.
- Path B: a producer cooperative or Majority-Controlled Producer-Based Business Venture owns the rig and cooler, with a veteran in the membership. It could take 5 + 5 = 10 points. However, the venture category is capped at 10% of funds and still has to show a value-added product. Custom slaughter as a fee-for-service probably does not qualify.
- Bottom line: 5-10 points out of the 100-point maximum in the regulation is a tie-breaker, not a door-opener.
