# NMPAN: Mobile slaughter/processing units currently in operation

Caveat in the page itself: "as more and more poultry MPUs come on line around the country, not all will be listed here." Dated notes inside entries: Sierra Foothills "last update: August 2012"; Taos Mobile Matanza "As of Fall 2015 ... not operating"; Glynwood MHS "As of 2015 ... no longer operating." [inferred] Most entries reflect about 2012-2015 and may be out of date.

## Poultry units: ownership and access model

| Unit | Owner | Regulatory basis | Access / rental model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sierra Foothills (Auburn, CA) | High Sierra Resource Conservation and Development Council | each producer under the 20,000 or 1,000 bird exemption | Shared; UC Cooperative Extension contact. Key challenge (2012): county health departments did not recognize unit as an "approved source," so marketing was limited |
| Kentucky MPPU (Frankfort, KY) | Kentucky State University Research Farm; KDA contact | "State-approved, but uninspected" | 200-250 birds/day; also rabbits and aquaculture |
| Island Grown, Martha's Vineyard (MA) | Island Grown Initiative (nonprofit) | 20,000 bird exemption | Shared program |
| Western MA, New England Small Farm Institute | NESFI (nonprofit) | state-approved, open-air, uninspected; producers need MA Dept. of Public Health slaughter license and local board of health approval | up to 300 birds/day; "a licensed-user mobile service-for-hire may be available" |
| Eastern MA, New Entry Sustainable Farming Project (Lowell) | New Entry (Tufts) | same licensing | "DIY for licensed users only; there is no mobile 'slaughter service' ... each producer/user must receive training and become state licensed to be eligible to rent the unit." Up to 400 birds/day |
| Montana Poultry Growers Cooperative (Ronan) | cooperative; Lake County Community Development contact | 20,000 bird exemption | 50-300 birds/day "depends on how much experience the user has" |
| Oregon Mobile Poultry Processing (Philomath) | LLP of two farmers | state-licensed, federal 20,000 exemption | private service, 50-500 birds/day |
| Parmelee Farm (VT) | private | custom | 20 birds/worker/hour |
| Tangletown Farm (VT) | farm bought unit from State of Vermont in 2012 | state-inspected | 250 birds/day; [inferred] example of a state-owned rental unit privatized |
| CADC (Colville, WA) | Community Agricultural Development Center (nonprofit) | 20,000 exemption; WSDA-approved unit | "As a local producer you can haul our WSDA-approved mobile poultry processing unit (MPPU) to your farm ... Training is required prior to use." 150-200 birds/day with 3-person crew |
| Pine Stump Farms / Slow Food Okanogan (Omak, WA) | farm + Slow Food chapter | WSDA-inspected; commercial sale incl. restaurants/retail | **$150/day + $200 damage deposit; $100/yr buy-in membership gives priority scheduling and discounted usage and deposit; or rent with crew at a central location for $5/bird** (year not stated; page circa 2012-2015) |

## Red-meat units (none rented DIY)
- Central Coast Grown (CA): owned by Central Coast Agriculture Cooperative, operated by J&R Meats (USDA cut-and-wrap); 5 beef or 10 hogs or 20 lambs/goats per day.
- Nebraska Prairie Harvest / Ranch Foods Direct (NE/CO): built by Nebraska Environmental Action Coalition "for $120,000 (includes the whole trailer and equipment but not the truck)"; owned by SRAP's Renewable Harvest, operated by Ranch Foods Direct; 10 beef/day; Renewable Harvest offers "free consulting" on building and operating MSUs.
- Taos County EDC Mobile Matanza (NM): county EDC owned; not operating as of Fall 2015.
- Glynwood Modular Harvest System (NY): owned/operated by LILA; not operating as of 2015.
- CADC (WA) livestock unit: owned by nonprofit, operated by S & K Processing (USDA cut-and-wrap); 6-7 beef/day.
- Alaska Meat Co., Univ. of Alaska reindeer unit, Wild Idea Buffalo, Broken Arrow Ranch, IGFC, PSMPC: owner-operated.

## Takeaways [inferred]
- Poultry rental works because federal/state producer exemptions let the bird owner be the slaughterer. Red meat has no equivalent exemption for sale, so red-meat units are leased to or run by an inspected operator rather than rented by the day to farmers.
- The "owner/nonprofit holds the asset, contracted processor runs it" pattern (Central Coast, CADC, Nebraska) is the closest red-meat analog to a rental program.
