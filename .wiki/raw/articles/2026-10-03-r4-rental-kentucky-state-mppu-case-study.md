# Kentucky Mobile Poultry Processing Unit (KSU), NMPAN case study, updated Sept. 2013

## Ownership and history
- Built 2001 by Heifer International with Kentucky State University (KSU), Partners for Family Farms (PFF), and NCAT. Total plan-and-build cost $70,000: $15,000 SARE seed (within a $100K pastured-poultry grant) + $55,000 raised by PFF and others.
- Immobilized soon after launch: regulators required wastewater treatment and an enclosed kill room. "under Heifer ownership, the cost of liability insurance was prohibitively expensive. Heifer sold the MPU to KSU in 2005 for $1."
- Regulatory status: "State-approved but uninspected." USDA ruled the 20,000-bird exemption "only applies to stationary plants, not to a facility used by multiple operators at multiple locations"; Heifer obtained "an exemption from the exemption" from USDA after KDA and the Cabinet for Health Services approved operations. Labels marked "Exempt Poultry P.L. 90-492."
- No business plan "beyond 'build it and they will come.'" Ten regular users (2013).

## Rental model
"Farmers use it on a rental basis to process poultry and aquaculture." Services: "rented to farmers to slaughter and process these species"; "Custom work: all custom." Max 7.5 hours/day of operation, "over that, user pays overtime." Capacity 200-250 birds/day depending on staffing.

## Price of services (2013)
- Chickens: $75 for first 50, $0.75 each additional
- Turkeys: $75 for first 15, $3.50 each additional
- Rabbits: $50 for first 50, $0.50 each additional
- "cost includes gas, water, electricity, aprons, booties, cleaning"
- Vacuum-pack bags $0.22 each
- Aquaculture: flat $75
- Training: "$75 for the first 2 years of training, $50 after that (every user, every two years)"
- Economics: "it breaks even: rental and training fees cover all operating costs except the coordinator, who is paid by KSU." One part-time coordinator (KSU employee).

## Training / certification of renters
"All MPU users must be trained every two years on how to use the MPU, including HACCP, SOPs, and SSOPs. The first half is in the classroom, the second half is processing birds. They then take a test, and if they pass, they are given a facility manager number, which must go on all their product labels and is good for two years. Without that number, they cannot process." Coordinator checks HACCP each use; Health Services is informed every use and attends many sessions; USDA checks the unit yearly and reviews HACCP paperwork. Training/use manual with HACCP plan sold "for a small fee."

## Insurance and waiver
"Because Heifer is a nonprofit organization, it had to require all users of the MPU to have $1 million in liability insurance. This was prohibitively expensive for many farmers. Under KSU ownership, users need only sign a waiver of liability each time they process, though they are advised to carry a product liability rider on their farm policy."

## Who tows / site requirements
- Unit is an enclosed 20' x 8' gooseneck cargo trailer; "must be pulled by at least a 3/4 ton pickup truck with a gooseneck ball and electrical connections." The coordinator "is regularly on the road, taking it between the three docking stations" (KSU Frankfort, Morehead State University, Jackson County). Poultry must be processed at a docking station; aquaculture can be done pond-side.
- Docking station: level pad; separate sloped concrete slaughter pad; 200-amp covered panel; approved water at 10+ gpm; 750-gal grease trap and 1,500-gal septic tank with fill alarm, pumped and hauled to a treatment plant; user brings a 100-lb propane tank (tanks cannot travel with the MPU); pest exclusion (pole barn at KSU). A farmer may build his own approved docking station, "but it is much less expensive to travel to the existing ones."
- Only NPIP-hatchery birds accepted; coordinator trained to condemn unhealthy birds.

## Relevance [inferred]
- Strongest documented evidence that renter insurance requirements kill rental demand: a $1M per-user liability requirement was "prohibitively expensive," and the fix was public (university) ownership plus a waiver. A private Virginia rental or consulting business would not get the public-entity benefit and should price in its own CGL/product coverage.
- Per-head fee schedule (base fee + per-unit increment) is a usable template for any per-animal rental or service price list.
