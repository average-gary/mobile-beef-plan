## Other education revenue

- **Custom education and travel teaching:** "Farmers and institutions book Brandon." Talks run "from one hour of time to one or more days." Rates are by email only, not published.
- **"Harvest classes":** "We specialize in making classes out of harvesting events for interested students near and far." In other words, a customer's on-farm custom slaughter becomes a teaching event (format: hands-on, on the farmer's own animal).
- **Membership:** "$9.99 a month" for harvest films, a harvest journal, and a resource library.

## Custom on-farm slaughter rates (FAQ)

- "If your pig weighs less than 200 lbs. you will be charged $160 ... If larger than 200 lbs. its $.80 /lb." (hanging weight). "The ball park figure for pig slaughter is between $300-$900 per pig" (this appears to include butchery [inferred]).
- Sheep: "$80/animal" minimum.
- "Slaughter harvest minimum ... of $300 is the minimum we charge for any slaughter work day."
- Deposit "$75" with a signed Service Agreement. The order form is due 48 hours before harvest.
- Travel: "$25/hour" round-trip travel time ("first hour is free") plus "$0.55 per mile (round trip)."
- Site requirements: the truck must park "within 5-10 feet" of penned animals. Animals must already be penned ("Paying us to chase sheep ... will make harvesting more expensive"). Withhold feed the morning of slaughter.
- "'On the hoof' refers to the wholesale of animals before they are killed to accommodate State requirements". This is the custom-exempt model: the owner buys the live animal before slaughter.

## Relevance [inferred]

- This is a working example of a mobile custom slaughterer that also earns education revenue on top of slaughter fees: paid classes, observer tickets, a membership, and speaking.
- Price points for a 1-day hands-on harvest class: $675-$775 per person. Multi-day classes run about $500/day.
- Turning a customer's own harvest into a "class" for the owner and guests is the cheapest version for a Shenandoah Valley operator, because no animal has to be bought. Whether paying students can attend a custom-exempt kill of someone else's animal under Virginia rules is an open question.
