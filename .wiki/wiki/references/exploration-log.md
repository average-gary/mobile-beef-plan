## 1. 2026-10-02: Research round 1, the landscape

- **Question:** what does it take to start a mobile beef slaughter and processing business in the Shenandoah Valley?
- **Short answer:** three regulatory models (custom-exempt, USDA MSU, state); inspected MSUs rarely pay on kill fees alone; the fixed cooler and cut room is the real dependency.
- **What changed:** 18 sources, 8 articles.
- **Links:** [[regulatory-models-compared|Regulatory Models Compared]] ([Regulatory Models Compared](../concepts/regulatory-models-compared.md)); [[fixed-facility-dependency|The Fixed-Facility Dependency]] ([The Fixed-Facility Dependency](../concepts/fixed-facility-dependency.md)).

## 2. 2026-10-02: Research round 2, closing gaps

- **Question:** close the open gaps (CIS, Virginia rules, disposal, rig cost, Valley prices, bottleneck) and find grants.
- **Short answer:** Virginia is not a CIS state; offal composting on the farm of kill is likely exempt; custom kill-only rigs are $72k-$115k (Sentinel 2026); the bottleneck is cutters, not kill floors.
- **What changed:** 30 sources, 4 new articles. Recommendation: kill-only rig + partner cut shop.
- **Links:** [[option-a-kill-only-partner-cut-shop|Option A]] ([Option A](../options/option-a-kill-only-partner-cut-shop.md)).

## 3. 2026-10-02: Research round 3, cooler space to back the mobile unit

- **Question:** what cooler space backs the mobile unit? Mid-round the model was set: the unit kills **and** cuts and packs; the fixed site is a hanging and aging cooler.
- **Short answer:** cutting labor becomes the ceiling (about 8 man-hours per custom beef); the cooler with a cutting dock is a slaughterhouse/processing use (RA CUP); blood and wash water are DEQ "industrial wastes".
- **What changed:** 23 sources, 3 new articles, business plan rewritten; business plan PDF and illustrative pro forma ($471,576 capex, break-even about 274 head/yr with a $50k owner draw). Option A superseded; Option B chosen.
- **Links:** [[option-b-cooler-dock-kill-and-cut|Option B]] ([Option B](../options/option-b-cooler-dock-kill-and-cut.md)).

## 4. 2026-10-03 (round 4): Zoning check of commercial and industrial districts

- **Question:** the wiki said a commercial parcel with public sewer "may be simpler". Is that true in Frederick County?
- **Short answer:** no. B2 (General Business) allows neither food manufacturing nor warehousing. "Animal Slaughtering and Processing" is by right **only in M2**; M1 and TM expressly exclude it. "Warehousing and Storage" is by right only in B3. No district offers either as a conditional use ([Article VI](../../raw/data/2026-10-03-r4-zoning-frederick-county-article-vi-use-lists.md)).
- **What changed:** cooler-site-permits, cooler-backed-mobile-model, the wiki business-plan topic and the dated plan output (with a correction notice; PDF regenerated) corrected; Option B's site answer is now "M2 by right or RA CUP".
- **Links:** [[option-b-cooler-dock-kill-and-cut|Option B]] ([Option B](../options/option-b-cooler-dock-kill-and-cut.md)); [[cooler-site-permits|Cooler Site Permits]] ([Cooler Site Permits](../concepts/cooler-site-permits.md)).

## 5. 2026-10-03 (round 4): Cut back on the farm instead of at the cooler

- **Question:** what if the cooler only stores, and the unit takes each carcass back to its farm to cut there?
- **Short answer:** the cooler becomes "Warehousing and Storage" (B3 by right) with no customer traffic, but throughput drops to about 5-7 head/week [inferred] against a break-even near 5.3 (274/52) [inferred], with double hauling and cutting wash water on every farm.
- **What changed:** new candidate Option C.
- **Links:** [[option-c-cooler-storage-cut-on-farm|Option C]] ([Option C](../options/option-c-cooler-storage-cut-on-farm.md)).

## 6. 2026-10-03 (round 4): Rent the unit to owners and offer storage

- **Question:** what if owners rent the mobile unit and do the work themselves, and the company offers cold storage?
- **Short answer:** legal for owners under personal use as long as the company does not "participate" (VDACS); fits the RA farmer-family-use exception. But the core freezer-beef market can't use personal use, no red-meat unit in NMPAN's directory (c. 2012-2015) is rented DIY, Virginia voids pre-injury waivers (Hiett 1992), and a 53 ft semi needs a CDL.
- **What changed:** new candidate Option D. Most of research round 4's sources were gathered here (20 in all across entries 4-7: 1 zoning, 4 rental, 4 storage, 4 rental insurance, 4 participation boundary, 3 education).
- **Links:** [[option-d-rental-plus-storage|Option D]] ([Option D](../options/option-d-rental-plus-storage.md)).

## 7. 2026-10-03 (round 4): Advise, consult and educate renters

- **Question** (*verbatim*): "What if we advised/consulted/educated people that were renting?"
- **Short answer:** every text found turns on **who performs** the slaughter or cutting, not on payment. Classroom and planning advice are very likely fine; hands-off coaching at a renter's kill is a gray zone (by analogy to Vermont, likely not "assisting" [inferred]; VDACS undefined); any hands-on act by staff makes the company a custom operator. Hold the free Custom Permit as a safe harbor. Benchmarks: $675-775 per student for a one-day hands-on harvest class (Farmstead Meatsmith 2026), $150 online courses (NMPAN 2025; MPA's is USDA-subsidized) and about $150/hr as MPA's implied value of bundled coaching (2025) [inferred], $75 renter certification (Kentucky 2013). Education adds about $35-45k/yr [placeholder]: a demand test and safety gate, not a debt carrier.
- **What changed:** new candidate Option E, the options hub, this log; 8 questions for VDACS OMPS.
- **Links:** [[option-e-rental-plus-education|Option E]] ([Option E](../options/option-e-rental-plus-education.md)).

## 8. 2026-10-03 (round 5): Combine the options into price tiers

- **Question** (*verbatim*): "there's likely some combination of offering as well that could be part of the price tiering. consider that in the final pass"
- **Short answer:** three tiers on the same unit and cooler, with add-ons: T1 Rent (Option D), T2 Coached (Option E, advise only) and T3 Full service (Option B, company Custom Permit). A tier is a legal configuration as well as a price. Hands-on staff work turns a T1 or T2 job into a custom-exempt job with the company as operator. Storing personal-use carcasses and verbal coaching at the kill need VDACS answers. Cutting at a B3 storage cooler is a zoning conflict in every tier. Pro forma results:
  - At default staffing, the 50/30/20 mix gives Y3 cash of $94,952 and break-even of 311 head with owner draw. Full service alone gives $129,507 and 274.
  - With payroll matched to the labor check, the 50/30/20 mix gives $120,495 and 274.
  - A DIY-heavy mix runs out of unit days (about 300 rental days at 375 head) before it runs out of staff hours [inferred].
  - Hypothesis: T3 is the core, and T1/T2 are an off-season and overflow tier at about 20-30% of head.
- **What changed:**
  - New candidate Option F, with a tier table, add-on price sheet and bundle compatibility matrix.
  - Calculator tier mix with three presets and a labor check (`site/proforma.js`, `site/assumptions.json`, tests).
  - Two round-5 sources: the Gore's Meats price tiers (the Frederick County incumbent, 2023 and 2026) and the Spring Hill VT operator-run unit (2009).
  - Gap still open: no U-cut facility or farm butchery co-op with published prices was found.
- **Links:** [[option-f-tiered-offering|Option F]] ([Option F](../options/option-f-tiered-offering.md)).
