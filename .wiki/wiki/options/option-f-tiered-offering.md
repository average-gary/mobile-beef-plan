# Option F: Tiered Offering (Rent, Coached, Full Service on One Unit and Cooler)

> **Status: candidate (new 2026-10-03, research round 5).** The question as posed: *"there's likely some combination
> of offering as well that could be part of the price tiering. consider that in the final pass"*. This option combines
> [[option-d-rental-plus-storage|Option D]] ([Option D](option-d-rental-plus-storage.md)) (rent),
> [[option-e-rental-plus-education|Option E]] ([Option E](option-e-rental-plus-education.md)) (coach) and
> [[option-b-cooler-dock-kill-and-cut|Option B]] ([Option B](option-b-cooler-dock-kill-and-cut.md)) (full service).
> See all options on [[business-model-options|Business Model Options]] ([Business Model Options](../topics/business-model-options.md)).
> **Not legal advice.** The tier boundaries below are a reading of the texts. Only VDACS OMPS can settle them.

## How it works

One unit, one cooler, one free Custom Permit held by the company. The owner chooses a tier per animal and adds options a la carte.

| Tier | What's included | Who does the work | Legal basis | Draws from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T1 Rent** | Unit for the day (company delivers and collects it, because a 53 ft semi needs a Class A CDL), certification before the first rental, carcass storage in the company cooler | **Owner** kills and cuts. Staff deliver, clean and inspect, and never touch the animal or carcass (the owner hauls and hangs the carcass; see workflow below) | Personal use, 9 CFR 303.1(a)(1). The closest VDACS text is the buyer-uses-facility sentence, which addresses a seller who is also the facility owner; applying it to a rental company is an analogy [inferred] (Option D). A producer selling shares instead uses **their own** free Custom Permit | [Option D](option-d-rental-plus-storage.md) |
| **T2 Coached** | T1, plus a pre-kill planning visit, a cut-sheet and aging plan, phone support, and hands-off coaching at the kill or cut | **Owner** does all physical work. Staff advise only (Option E levels 0-3) | As T1. Level 3 (verbal coaching at the kill) is a **gray zone** | [Option E](option-e-rental-plus-education.md) |
| **T3 Full service** | Farm-call kill, hang and age in the cooler, cut, vac-pack, label "Not for Sale", owner pickup | **Company staff** | Company's VDACS Custom Permit, which covers a "mobile slaughter and processing unit" | [Option B](option-b-cooler-dock-kill-and-cut.md) (or [Option C](option-c-cooler-storage-cut-on-farm.md) if the cooler is storage only) |

**T1/T2 workflow with cooler storage.** The owner cannot cut in the unit after the carcass leaves for the cooler, so a stored T1/T2 head takes one of two routes:
- (a) Kill on the farm on day 1. The owner hauls the carcass to the cooler and hangs it. After aging, the unit returns to the farm and the owner hauls the carcass back to cut. That is **2 unit days per head** [inferred].
- (b) The owner cuts at the company cooler dock. That is only possible at an M2 site, and it needs VDACS confirmation (see the matrix row on cutting at the dock).
- With no storage, the owner kills and cuts in one unit day and takes the carcass home.

**Off-season variant (Feb-Apr):** T1 and T2 for hogs, sheep and goats, as in Option D's variant. The RA family-use exception names "cattle, sheep, swine, goats and fowl" ([RA](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r3-coolreg-frederick-county-zoning-ra.md)).

Price-structure precedents:
- **Gore's (Frederick County incumbent):** a base kill fee plus a per-lb cut rate, with labor-heavy upcharges and storage billed only after a free window ([Gore's](../../raw/data/2026-10-03-r5-tiers-gores-meats-frederick-shenandoah-price-tiers.md)). In 2023 its ladder also had a kill-and-age-only tier ($105) and primals with no wrap ($0.70/lb). Neither appears on the 2026 web pages.
- **Spring Hill (VT, operator-run poultry unit, 2009):** a base per-head fee, per-unit packaging add-ons, one-way mileage, and a per-stop minimum that small producers could meet by pooling at a booked site ([Spring Hill](../../raw/articles/2026-10-03-r5-tiers-nmpan-spring-hill-tangletown-vt-mppu-operator-model.md)).

## Legal basis

**A tier is a legal configuration, not only a price.** Every text found turns on **who physically performs** the slaughter or processing, not on payment [inferred] (Option E).
- VDACS: "If the seller participates in the slaughter or processing activity, then the facility owner is subject to the custom exempt criteria and must have a Custom Permit." "Participates" is not defined ([VDACS](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-reg-vdacs-red-meat-custom-exemptions.md)).
- Vermont, by analogy only: providing a site, implements or offal disposal is not "assisting" ([Vermont](../../raw/articles/2026-10-03-r4-participation-vermont-6vsa-3311a-personal-slaughter.md)).
- Consequence [inferred]: **any hands-on staff act on a T1 or T2 job makes that job custom-exempt with the company as the custom operator, so it is legally a T3 job.** That includes stunning, sticking, running the winch, a demonstration cut and SRM removal. The company's own Custom Permit is the safe harbor ([8160.1](../../raw/articles/2026-10-03-r4-participation-fsis-directive-8160-1-custom-exempt-review.md)). The job must then meet the custom criteria: owner-household use, "Not for Sale", and records. It should also be billed as T3.
- **Storage differs by tier.** T3 carcasses are custom product (owned by the animal's owner) that the company holds as custom operator; whether the Custom Permit covers the cooler is Option B open question 3. T1 and T2 carcasses are owner-held, uninspected personal-use product. Holding them for a fee may fall in the VDACS **Meat and Poultry Handlers Permit** "public warehouse" class or **§ 3.2-5404** registration ([Handlers Permit](../../raw/articles/2026-10-03-r4-storage-vdacs-meat-poultry-handlers-permit.md); [§ 3.2-5404](../../raw/articles/2026-10-03-r4-storage-va-code-3-2-5404-warehouseman-registration.md)). Whether the two kinds may share one cooler, and how they must be kept apart, is **open from round 4**.
- "Own raising" gap: a T1 renter who bought the animal does not fit the federal text literally ([9 CFR 303.1](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-reg-9cfr-303-1-custom-exemption.md)). Get VDACS's position in writing.

### Bundle compatibility matrix

Key:
- **Clean:** fits the stated basis [inferred].
- **Flips to custom:** the job becomes custom-exempt with the company as operator, so bill it and run it as T3.
- **VDACS:** needs written OMPS confirmation.
- **Zoning:** conflicts with Frederick County use lists.

| T3 + cooler storage, extra storage, dry-aging | **VDACS** | Whether the Custom Permit covers the cooler is Option B open question 3. The Handlers Permit source also lists a locker holding only owner-held custom-exempt product as an open OMPS question |

| Same unit used for T3 on some days and T1/T2 rentals on others | **VDACS** | Option E question 5: sanitation and records for each use |
| T1/T2 renter cuts **at the company cooler dock** | **Zoning** (plus VDACS) | Cutting is "Animal Slaughtering and Processing": M2 by right or an RA CUP only, so not at a B3 storage cooler. At an M2 site the closest VDACS text is the buyer-uses-facility sentence, which addresses a seller/facility owner; applying it to a rental company is an analogy [inferred] |

## Zoning and site (Frederick County)

The tiers do not change the site answer. They inherit it ([Article VI](../../raw/data/2026-10-03-r4-zoning-frederick-county-article-vi-use-lists.md); [RA](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r3-coolreg-frederick-county-zoning-ra.md)):
- **M2 site (Option B):** all three tiers are possible. Storing T1/T2 carcasses in the same cooler is part of the processing building [inferred, ask the Zoning Administrator].
- **B3 storage-only site (Options C/D):** "Warehousing and Storage" and trailer rental are by right, but no cutting can happen on site, in any tier. T3 becomes Option C (cut back on the farm). The 53 ft semi's rental classification is still uncertain.
- **Farm side:** T1/T2 fit the RA farmer-family-use exception when the owner raised the animal. T3 carries Option B's open question (is a farm kill a "slaughterhouse" use?).

## Waste

- **T1:** offal stays on the renter's farm (9VAC20-81-95 D.4) ([DEQ](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r2-disposal-deq-vswmr-exemptions-compost.md)). The rental agreement requires blood capture and a holding tank. The pro forma charges no rendering.
- **T2:** as T1, or the company hauls offal as an add-on. The model's `renderingCoached` = 1 assumes it does, and that legal status still needs confirming.
- **T3:** as Option B. Dock wash water goes to sewer at an M2 site.

**What mix keeps break-even feasible [inferred]:**
- Full service alone needs about 274 head with owner draw, within Option B's 300-400 head/yr cutter capacity.
- At default staffing, every DIY share pushes break-even up, to 311 head at 50/30/20 and 373 at 20/30/50.

**Price-gap check [inferred]:**
- **T2 vs T3.** T2 ($825) is only $105 below T3 ($930), yet the owner does all the work. The gap is probably too small to draw buyers who don't want to learn, so T2 has to sell on learning, on scheduling (no wait for a T3 slot), or a lower coaching fee for repeat renters.
- **T1 vs Gore's.** T1 ($450) compares with Gore's 2023 $105 kill-and-age tier plus the farmer's own trucking and cutting. That is the local anchor.

## What it fixes

- Uses one capital stack (unit + cooler + permit) to serve three customer types: owners who want it done, owners who want to learn, and owners who want it cheap.
- Moves head away from the cutter ceiling, Option B's binding constraint, and fills the off-season.
- Gives Options D and E a home as tiers rather than standalone businesses, which is how their verdicts already framed them.
- The company's Custom Permit makes a T1/T2 job that slips into hands-on work a permitted custom job rather than an unpermitted one [inferred], provided the job meets the custom criteria (records, an owner list made before slaughter, "Not for Sale").

## What it costs

- **Compliance discipline:** every job needs a recorded tier before the kill. If staff go hands-on, the job is re-tiered and re-billed as T3 ("hands in pockets" for T1/T2).
- **Two storage regimes** in one cooler until VDACS answers the Handlers Permit question.
- **Liability:** amateurs on company equipment. Virginia voids pre-injury personal-injury waivers (*Hiett*, 1992) ([Hiett](../../raw/articles/2026-10-03-r4-rentins-hiett-va-preinjury-waiver-void.md)). Spring Hill's successor closed its unit to outside users "due to liability risk" (2018) ([Spring Hill](../../raw/articles/2026-10-03-r5-tiers-nmpan-spring-hill-tangletown-vt-mppu-operator-model.md)).
- Lower revenue per head on DIY tiers. Cash suffers unless payroll is actually cut.
- The unit calendar is shared, so DIY rental days compete with T3 kill and cut days.
- Price and menu complexity for a small operator.

## Open questions

1. VDACS OMPS: Option E's questions 1-7, especially storage of owner-held personal-use carcasses next to custom product, and dual use of one unit.
2. VDACS: in a split job (staff kill, owner cuts), is the owner's cutting still personal use?
3. Zoning Administrator: storage of T1/T2 carcasses at an M2 processing cooler; semi rental classification in B3.
4. Demand: what share of Valley owners would pick T1/T2 over T3, and at what price gap?
5. A red-meat rental-day price (none found). Does a pooled multi-head rental day work?
6. Broker quote: rental-equipment GL and inland marine with renter additional-insured certificates.

## Verdict

**Candidate. Working hypothesis [inferred]:** keep T3 full service as the core (at least about 70% of head). Offer T1 and T2 as an **off-season and overflow tier**, capped at about 20-30% of head, priced per rental day with pooled stops. Gate it on certification, and run it only after written VDACS answers on storage and coaching. Match payroll to the labor check so DIY head does not carry T3 staffing.

**What would change it:**
- VDACS says level-3 coaching is participation: drop T2 to classroom and planning only.
- VDACS requires a separate permit or a separate cooler for personal-use storage: price that cost into T1/T2, or drop storage from those tiers.
- Demand tests show strong DIY interest: raise the T1/T2 cap and add a towable second unit.
- T3 cutters can't be hired: shift the mix toward T1/T2 by necessity.
- Only a B3 site is available: T3 becomes Option C, and the tiers still apply.
