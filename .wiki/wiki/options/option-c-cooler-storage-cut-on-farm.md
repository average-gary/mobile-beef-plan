# Option C: Storage-Only Cooler, Cut Back on the Farm

> **Status: candidate.** Explored 2026-10-03 after the zoning check showed that a cooler with a cutting
> dock is by right only in M2. All options:
> [[business-model-options|Business Model Options]] ([Business Model Options](../topics/business-model-options.md)). Not legal advice.

## How it works

1. Kill on the owner's farm, as in [[option-b-cooler-dock-kill-and-cut|Option B]] ([Option B](option-b-cooler-dock-kill-and-cut.md)).
2. The carcass hangs 7-21 days in the company cooler, which **only stores**: no cutting, no customers.
3. The mobile unit collects the aged carcass and **cuts and packs it back on the farm of origin**; the owner takes the boxes there.

## Legal basis

- Same free VDACS Custom Permit. VDACS allows split stages: an owner may move custom product between custom facilities if it is marked "Not for Sale" (custom guide, Note 13) ([VDACS](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-reg-vdacs-red-meat-custom-exemptions.md)).
- Storage of others' carcasses for a fee may need a free, 3-year VDACS **Meat and Poultry Handlers Permit** ("public warehouses") under Va. Code § 3.2-5404(10). The guide is written for inspected, labelled product and is silent on custom-exempt carcasses: **ask OMPS** ([Handlers Permit](../../raw/articles/2026-10-03-r4-storage-vdacs-meat-poultry-handlers-permit.md); [§ 3.2-5404](../../raw/articles/2026-10-03-r4-storage-va-code-3-2-5404-warehouseman-registration.md)). If the cooler is part of the custom operation itself, the Custom Permit may cover it instead [inferred].

## Zoning and site (Frederick County)

From the Article VI use lists ([Article VI](../../raw/data/2026-10-03-r4-zoning-frederick-county-article-vi-use-lists.md)):

- **B3 Industrial Transition: "Warehousing and Storage" by right.** The cleanest fit.
- **M1: not listed** as a named use; it would need the Zoning Administrator to classify a carcass cooler under a listed use. Treat as unconfirmed. M2 inherits M1's list, so the same gap applies there.
- TM: warehousing only as a secondary use on a 10-acre-plus site. B2: no warehousing.
- Whether hanging an owner's uninspected carcass for a fee is NAICS 493120 refrigerated warehousing is a classification call for the Zoning Administrator.
- No processing on site, so § 165-204.17 (slaughterhouse setbacks) likely does not apply [inferred].
- No customer pickup at the cooler, which keeps it far below the VDH 25-person waterworks threshold ([12VAC5-590](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r3-coolreg-12vac5-590-waterworks-thresholds.md)).
- Farm side: the open RA question for all options (is a mobile unit killing *and now cutting* on RA farms a "slaughterhouse" use?) gets sharper here because cutting also happens on farms ([RA](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r3-coolreg-frederick-county-zoning-ra.md)).

## Waste

- Offal and blood: as Option B (D.4 composting on the farm of kill; capture blood) ([DEQ](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r2-disposal-deq-vswmr-exemptions-compost.md)).
- **New problem:** cutting wash water (fat and meat solids, no blood) is generated on every farm. On the VPA text it is "industrial waste" ([VPA](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r3-water-9vac25-32-vpa-scope-exclusions.md)). Needs an onboard holding tank and a legal disposal route (septage hauler or a sewer dump station) [inferred].
- The cooler itself produces only light wash-down water.

## Capacity and labor

- Each farm visit pays travel plus setup, teardown and wash-down, and most owners have 1-3 head.
- [inferred] About **1-2 carcasses per cut day** instead of 2-3 at a dock, so about **5-7 head/week**, against a break-even of about 5.3/week (274/52) [inferred] (274 head/yr from the Option B pro forma). The margin of safety mostly disappears.
- Cutting labor rule unchanged: about 8 man-hours per custom beef (NMPAN/Lorentz 2010) ([NMPAN costing](../../raw/articles/2026-10-02-r3-cutshop-nmpan-costing-throughput-cutter-productivity.md)).

## Economics

- Capex: close to Option B's $471,576 (same kill-and-cut unit) [inferred], plus a holding tank; possibly less site cost if a B3 lease is cheaper than M2 (not checked).
- Revenue per head: as Option B (about $800-1,100) [inferred], with more mileage billed.
- Costs up: double hauling (farm, cooler, farm), fuel, more unit hours per head, wash-water disposal.
- No pro forma run for this option yet [placeholder].

## What it fixes

- Avoids the M2-only processing use and the RA slaughterhouse CUP at the cooler.
- No customer traffic at the cooler.
- Cooler could be a simpler storage building.

## What it costs

- Lower throughput, right at break-even.
- Double hauling and a cold chain at 40°F or below on each move.
- Farm-by-farm cutting wash water.
- Moves processing onto RA farms, which sharpens the open farm-side zoning question.

## Open questions

1. Zoning Administrator: is a fee-based carcass cooler "Warehousing and Storage" in B3? Could M1 work?
2. OMPS: Handlers Permit, Custom Permit, or both for the storage cooler?
3. Septage hauler or dump-station cost for cutting wash water per farm visit.
4. Actual cut-day throughput with setup and wash-down on farms (needs a timed trial).

## Verdict

**Candidate, weaker than B on throughput.** Worth keeping if M2 sites prove unaffordable or unavailable; otherwise the dock wins on labor efficiency.
